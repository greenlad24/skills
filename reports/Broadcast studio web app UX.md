# Build Sunrise Jam as a Watch-and-Override Cockpit

Sunrise Jam should feel like StreamYard but behave like a control room run by Claude. That means one "Show" screen built around a large program monitor, a row of phone-camera tiles, a six-fader mixer, and a right-hand AI activity sidebar that collapses to an icon rail. Two controls are always visible on that screen: an off-white **Take manual control** button and a mode badge that says what the AI is running. Everything a professional needs is real but one layer down: preview/program with CUT and AUTO, a full 16-channel engineer mixer with EQ and dynamics editors, encoder health and raw logs. This split is what the evidence supports. The tools reviewers call easiest ship a preset stage, one-click layouts and a single Go Live button rather than an empty canvas ([V: 135k, C: 3 channels](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MDx8fKnU3Sk)). The most-praised mixer apps show "only what this show uses" ([V: 353k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bGcOnbUFjA0)). Trust in automation comes from visible status, explanations, cheap undo and global controls ([GUIDELINE: Microsoft HAX](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/library/pattern/)). The product also goes one step past the market. No streaming product reviewed shows *why* its auto-switcher cut ([C: 3 channels](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4HRtjeAQDks)), so the AI's next shot sits in the green preview slot with a one-line reason. The colour system follows your decisions, which override the research conventions. **ON AIR is blue, preview is green, warnings are amber, and red means errors only**, all on a warm near-black "sunrise" surface. Every semantic colour passes WCAG contrast on the canvas (5.9:1 to 10.8:1, computed below). Output is fixed at 1080p60, and the break slate counts down from 15:00 by default.

## Easy tools ship a stage, not a blank canvas, and Sunrise Jam has one screen that matters

The strongest signal from 39 tutorial and review transcripts is that "easy" means "pre-built." StreamYard's studio is "your home base": a stage with sources in a "backstage" tray underneath, one-click layout buttons and one blue Go Live button ([V: 135k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MDx8fKnU3Sk)). Restream's dashboard "essentially just walks you right into the studio" ([C: 2 channels](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fNn6HbDIrlE)). Mevo got a reviewer "up and running in less than five minutes," tapping phones to switch angles live ([V: 450k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0ksEvsSH0oc)). The pain points mirror this. OBS opens to a black canvas and a scene/source model that the 6.3M-view beginner guide skips as too advanced ([V: 6.3M](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTk99mHDX_I)). ATEM hides preview/program mode in a separate setup app ([V: 337k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C4ERujxrXk4)). Mevo and StreamYard silently default to 480p and 720p ([C: 2 channels](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqGbyMnrpG4)). Phone switchers take audio from the wrong device ([V: 142k, vendor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MlobdIgwxj0)). Every one of these traps is a design rule for Sunrise Jam. Never show an empty canvas. Never hide a show-critical mode in settings. Default to the real target, 1080p60, not a safe low setting. Keep one audio source of truth, the mixer, never per-scene audio.

Because the AI runs everything in AUTO by default, the information architecture inverts the usual studio. Normal software is a set of tools the operator drives. Here the primary screen is a dashboard the human *watches*, and tools appear only when the human reaches in. The levels-of-automation literature says each function can sit at its own level ([GUIDELINE: Parasuraman/Sheridan](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA461061.pdf)). IBM's "on the loop" model fits your decision exactly: the AI acts autonomously while a human monitors and keeps "veto power to hit the kill switch or override" ([IBM Technology, 48k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9iS-YYLIXiw); [C: 2+ sources with Smashing's "Autonomy Dial"](https://smashingmagazine.com/2026/02/designing-agentic-ai-practical-ux-patterns/)). The proposed structure below is an **inference** built from those patterns.

| Area | Default view (watch) | Reveal on demand (override / engineer) | Primary evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Show** (home, 90% of the time) | Program monitor (blue when live), AI-proposed preview (green), camera tiles with health, 6 group faders, LIVE timer, viewers, stream health, mode badge, Take manual control | CUT/AUTO/T-bar, number-key preview, layout buttons | StreamYard stage [V: 135k]; ATEM/vMix multiview [V: 286k] |
| **Cameras** | Onboarding QR, backstage tray of joining phones, per-phone health | Framing presets, WB/exposure values (locked per stream), re-pair | VDO.Ninja QR flow [V: 427k]; Mevo Go roles and locks [V: 325k] |
| **Mix** | Simple mode: band-section strips, meters, mute, AI badges, IEM chips | Engineer mode: 16 inputs, buses, FX, EQ/dynamics editors, snapshots | Mixing Station [V: 353k]; Ui24R [V: 185k] |
| **Show flow** | Pre-flight checklist, Go-live countdown, Break slate (15:00), End/outro | Edit run-of-show, break length, test stream | StreamYard go-live [V: 135k]; YouTube test streams [V: 414k] |
| **AI sidebar** (right, every screen) | 56 px rail: process icons + status dots + mode badge | 340 px panel: autonomy, process health, activity feed; "Open full log" route | HAX G11/G17/G18 [GUIDELINE]; Kole Jain sidebar [V: 361k] |
| **After show** | Upload/recording progress, "don't close yet", learning summary | Per-decision log, replay A/B | Switcher post-stop state [V: 142k, vendor] |

The single most important architectural choice follows from the cross-cutting finding that every easy tool keeps its power layer *off by default*. Studio Mode is toggled off for beginners ([V: 1.6M](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9z9GiEM4uvA); [C: 2 with Prism, 276k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LxkmWXR6dVs)), and OBS output "simple mode... will work for most of you" ([V: 6.3M](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DTk99mHDX_I)). Sunrise Jam departs from this in one place. Preview/program is always on in the Show screen, because the preview slot is where the AI shows what it is about to do. Everything else that is engineer-grade (the T-bar, transitions, encoder settings, per-channel processing) sits behind an explicit **Engineer** toggle.

## The director screen shows program, the AI's next shot and every phone at a glance

### Multiview: blue program, green AI preview, numbered phone tiles

The professional multiview standard is "preview and program on top, up to 8 inputs below," with CUT swapping the two, AUTO running a timed transition and a T-bar for manual fades ([V: 286k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sFH-dT2s02w); [C: 2 with ATEM-KR, 337k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C4ERujxrXk4)). The vMix multiview adds coloured banners, audio bars and input numbers on each tile, plus "click to select input" ([vMix, 45k, vendor, UNVERIFIED](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tD--1IMzsJU)). Consumer apps skip preview and cut on tap ([C: 3 channels: Mevo, Switcher, Cinamaker](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MlobdIgwxj0)). On Dribbble, the highest-engagement studio concept uses the same skeleton: a large program view top-centre at roughly 55% width with labelled control panels in a band beneath ([Dribbble: uixNinja, 537 likes / 203k views](https://dribbble.com/shots/22597564-OBS-Studio-concept-with-AI-and-happy-song)).

For Sunrise Jam the program tile carries a **3 px blue border and a blue "LIVE" pill**, replacing the red pill that every Dribbble streaming shot uses ([Dribbble: GoLive, 522 / 166k](https://dribbble.com/shots/18627261-GoLive-Streaming-App)) and the broadcast convention that "program is always red" ([V: 286k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sFH-dT2s02w)). This is your decision, and it has a measurable benefit. The research notes had to invent a workaround because red would otherwise mean both "live" and "broken" ([GUIDELINE-derived inference, NN/g + HAX](https://nngroup.com/articles/indicators-validations-notifications)). Blue removes that collision entirely. It also separates program from preview more clearly for red-green colour-blind viewers, who confuse red and green but not blue and green (**inference**). One cost needs managing. Musicians and camera operators who know broadcast will expect a red tally, so the phone tally always pairs the blue fill with the word "LIVE."

The preview tile has a green border, and in AUTO it holds **the AI's intended next shot with a reason and a countdown**: "Next: Cam 3 Keys, solo detected · 3 s · Veto." That gives HAX feedforward ("16A: convey the consequences... before") and a veto window without blocking the show ([GUIDELINE: HAX](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/library/pattern/)). No product reviewed does this. Ecamm's auto-switching is "kind of a dumb switching situation" on timers ([UNVERIFIED, 22k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4HRtjeAQDks)). Mevo's Auto Director is a star for a priority camera plus a dwell slider ([V: 117k, sponsored](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqGbyMnrpG4)). The AI preview is therefore a differentiator, not a proven pattern (**inference**). Copy Mevo's simple controls (priority camera, minimum and maximum dwell) into the Director's settings popover. Copy Ecamm's override rule too: any manual cut overrides the auto-switcher, which then "pick[s] up where you left off" ([UNVERIFIED, 22k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4HRtjeAQDks)).

Below program and preview sits one row of camera tiles: 16:9, 12 px radius, the phone's name ("Cam 3 · Keys"), a mini audio meter and corner health chips. ATEM's operator names on multiview tiles make it "much easier for the TD to know who they're working with" ([UNVERIFIED, 55k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iLu4IoB16GE)). Corner chips in frosted pills follow the Sentry camera-monitoring shot ([Dribbble: Phenomenon, 191 / 38.8k](https://dribbble.com/shots/26952820-Live-Camera-Monitoring-Mobile-UI-Sentry)). Hotkeys mirror pro switchers: 1–9 send a tile to preview, Space is CUT, Enter is AUTO. Hotkeys plus Stream Deck support appear in OBS, Ecamm and Prism ([C: 3 channels](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LxkmWXR6dVs)). Any key press is a manual action and logs as such in the sidebar. Add an ATEM-style **Fade to Black that also fades audio**. It prevents "embarrassing moments... when you think you've gone offline but the audio is actually still alive" ([UNVERIFIED, 55k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iLu4IoB16GE)).

### Camera onboarding: scan, choose a role, lock, and appear in backstage

The easiest join flows need no account. Scan a QR code, allow permissions, pick the rear camera, rotate and start ([V: 427k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=86S0tV1QeCE); [C: 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SmcrEvXxmlE)). Mevo Go adds a role choice (video+audio, screencast, mic only), a hold-to-unlock **lock button** so bystanders cannot change settings, focus and exposure locks, and a warning when Wi-Fi load rises ([V: 325k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nPBdWM7Z158)). Switcher lets the director remotely set and lock zoom, focus and exposure ([V: 142k, vendor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MlobdIgwxj0)).

Under your decisions, the Cameras screen shows one QR code. On iPhone it opens the Safari camera page; on Android it deep-links to the native app. The phone screen asks three things in order: role, name (pre-filled "Cam 3 – Drums") and "Plug in power" confirmation. StreamYard's own phone tips are to plug in, use a tripod and keep a strong signal ([UNVERIFIED, vendor, 55k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-UqUffUWEoo)), and Mevo warns the app "has to be loaded and active... you can't take a phone call" ([C: 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2ZCpRhIPflY)). The phone then shows a big lock and, when its camera is on program, a blue full-width "LIVE" bar. Mevo's stealth LEDs avoid distracting performers ([V: 117k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqGbyMnrpG4)), so offer a dimmed tally option. On the director side, the joining phone appears in a backstage tray with Accept and Reject. This is StreamYard's "add to stage" flow ([V: 135k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MDx8fKnU3Sk)), and in AUTO the AI accepts phones that pass health checks and logs it.

White balance and exposure are locked per stream, by your decision. Show this as a small lock chip on each tile ("WB 5600 K · EXP 🔒"). The lock is set once during pre-flight and cannot be changed by the AI mid-show. Changing it needs a human hold-to-confirm, because a mid-song colour shift on one camera breaks the multicam look (**inference**).

### Health shows on the tile, with numbers one click away

Mevo's per-camera menu shows Wi-Fi, battery and storage, with a disclosure triangle for bitrate and a latency setting ([V: 117k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqGbyMnrpG4)). YouTube Live Control Room has a "Stream health" tab ([V: 1.5M, vendor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RAo2gN3K3GQ)), and Prism shows health in its bottom bar with an up-arrow for detail ([C: 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LxkmWXR6dVs)). OBS's dynamic bitrate trades quality for no dropped frames ([V: 198k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MVwyFNZzxic)). Geckoboard warns that "if your entire dashboard is red and green, then the significance of those colors is greatly reduced" ([V: 296k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t3cAUt7sOQg)).

Each tile therefore shows *nothing* when healthy except a single neutral dot. It raises an amber chip ("Battery 18%", "Wi-Fi weak", "54 fps") or a red chip ("Disconnected", "Format mismatch") only on a problem. Click the dot for the full panel: battery and charging, Wi-Fi RSSI, bitrate, fps against the 60 target, thermal state, latency, and the locked WB/exposure values. The research found no high-view video showing live battery or thermal *alerts*, so these alerts are an inferred need, not an observed pattern ([gap noted against MEVO-PJ](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqGbyMnrpG4)). When a phone drops, the AI should cut away first and alert second. Mevo's "auto wide on app disconnect" is the precedent ([V: 117k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqGbyMnrpG4)).

### Go-live, break and end run themselves but announce every step

The best go-live flow observed is StreamYard's. Destination, title and privacy are set first, then you enter the studio with a device check. A confirmation repeats broadcast quality and recording toggles. Once live, a LIVE badge and elapsed timer show top-left, and **End needs a second confirmation** "so you don't accidentally end it" ([V: 135k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MDx8fKnU3Sk); [C: 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YlcyoE3q7zY)). A private or unlisted test stream is the standard rehearsal ([V: 414k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6cVEfihXNnk); [C: 2+](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Dxa3BJCyb6Q)). No reviewed tool has a formal pre-flight checklist, so the one below is assembled from those fragments (**inference**).

In AUTO, the AI owns this flow, and the UI turns the confirmation dialog into a visible plan. The Show flow screen shows a checklist that ticks itself green item by item, in the same way GitHub's Copilot agent ticks off its plan ([GitHub, 75k, below threshold but consistent with HAX G11/G18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1GVBRhDI5No)). The items are: server started, YouTube event and privacy, **1080p60** encoder confirmed, each phone connected with battery at least 50% or charging, mixer receiving all 16 channels with peaks checked, recording on, AI director and mixer armed. At the scheduled time the header reads "Going live in 0:30 · **Hold**". That gives a feedforward veto (HAX 16A) in place of a blocking modal. At zero the "Starting soon" slate plays, then program cuts to the opening shot. If any item is red, the AI does not go live. It holds and escalates (HAX 10C, "fall back to other strategies"). For a human-initiated end, keep StreamYard's two-step confirmation and offer an "Outro / thanks" slate first.

The break screen combines Mevo's one-tap "be right back" overlay ([V: 325k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nPBdWM7Z158)) with Ecamm's countdown that auto-advances to the next scene ([UNVERIFIED, 22k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4HRtjeAQDks)). It reads "We'll be back in…" above a **15:00** default countdown in 160 px tabular numerals on the sunrise gradient. In AUTO the AI triggers it from the run-of-show or on a detected long pause. Program audio crossfades to break music and the stage mics fade down, so there is no hot mic. At 0:00 the AI returns to a wide shot and the house mix. A human can extend the break (+5 min), end it now, or edit the default. After the show, keep a "Finishing upload — don't close" state, as Switcher requires after Stop ([V: 142k, vendor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MlobdIgwxj0)). Show recording, upload and post-show learning as processes in the sidebar.

## The mixing station hides 16 channels behind six faders until the engineer asks

Engineers praise Mixing Station for three reasons: it uses "the same layout... no matter which mixer," it lets users set "channels per layer" so six inputs get "nice big faders," and its "fit channels" option resizes strips to the screen ([V: 353k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bGcOnbUFjA0); [C: 2 channels](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iGphpo6D8Pg)). The browser-based Ui24R proves a web mixer is credible: "every device is a web browser," and it is "astoundingly stable" with many simultaneous clients ([V: 158k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uThRC8GMmD8); [C: 2 with 185k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=np5pDM1a5eQ)). Simple-versus-advanced splits are common. The Ui24R's "Easy EQ" collapses to bass/mid/treble ([V: 185k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=np5pDM1a5eQ)), and the DM3 has single-knob compression ([V: 119k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dwjwxDjmOBA)). The top complaint even about flagship consoles is lag and "99 ways to do the same thing" ([UNVERIFIED, 30k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tqWTsCC9s78)).

**Simple mode** (the default) shows one strip per band section (Drums, Bass, Keys, Guitar, Horns/Solo, Vocals) plus Audience and the Stream master. Each strip has an instrument icon as its label, a large fader with a dB readout, a segmented meter, mute, and a small lilac "AI" dot when the AI touched it in the last 30 s. The master strip adds a LUFS readout against the project's −16 LUFS / −1 dBTP stream target, which comes from the band's mix-chain spec ([C: 2 channels, 109k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2jhggSKR2AI)). One macro knob per strip ("Presence", "Tightness") gives non-engineers a single control. The Dribbble model is X-Vox: four colour-coded modules with power and solo, one big "WIDE · 50%" macro and global input/output LED meters ([Dribbble: Voicu Apostol, 397 / 117k](https://dribbble.com/shots/20314717-Neumorphic-Audio-Plugin)). Arc-ring knobs, segmented meters and a colour per channel recur across four designers each ([Dribbble: Ofspace, 329 / 58.8k](https://dribbble.com/shots/6138741-Audio-Plugin); [Layo, 526 / 142k](https://dribbble.com/shots/19695957-Audio-equalizer-dashboard-concept)). The only literal "simple mixer for creators" shot uses app icons as channel labels but has low engagement, so use it for structure only ([Dribbble: Serhii Huba, 17 / 6.8k, below threshold](https://dribbble.com/shots/25785310-Audio-Mixer-Interface-for-B2C-Creator-SaaS)).

**Engineer mode** shows all 16 inputs, the buses, FX returns and the master on one fit-to-width layer, with no paging. Strips are named and coloured by section, because users of the DM3 colour-code by category ([V: 119k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dwjwxDjmOBA)). Tapping a strip opens Mixing Station's two-tier view: an overview with mini HPF, gate, EQ and compressor graphs, where each graph opens its full editor, and prev/next arrows step between channels ([V: 353k, C: 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bGcOnbUFjA0)). Use the touch conventions both Mixing Station and the Ui24R use: double-tap resets, long-press opens typed fine entry, and a corner glyph marks hold actions ([C: 2 channels, V: 185k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=np5pDM1a5eQ)). The EQ editor copies FabFilter Pro-Q, whose UI "has to be good for your workflow." It has drag nodes with a preview of the band about to be created, plus an overlay of competing channels' curves with collision highlighting ([V: 127k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tejnPHku378)). Dynamics get big draggable threshold lines, which a reviewer calls "a really, really nice way of working" on a tablet ([V: 158k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uThRC8GMmD8)). For IEM work, sends-on-fader tints the whole background in the bus colour. That is "a good way to not accidentally forget... I'm on my ears" ([V: 353k, C: 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bGcOnbUFjA0)). Bus tints must avoid blue, green, amber and red; use the channel palette (**inference**). Mixing Station's high-contrast themes exist for "playing outside in really bright environments where dark mode isn't really working" ([V: 353k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bGcOnbUFjA0)). A sunrise show may well be outdoors, so ship an **Outdoor** high-contrast light theme.

Every AI mixer tool that reviewers liked made its change a visible, editable object rather than a black box. Sonible's smart band can be made "more extreme, less extreme, inverted (so disagree with it)" ([V: 102k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I2FHV3li314)). Neutron users correct misidentified instruments and compare manual and auto snapshots ([V: 162k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zOxo81pijoU)). Three channels agree that AI output is a "starting point" that a human must be able to read, tweak and A/B at matched level ([C: 3 channels, V: 162k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WrdGPGChsng)). So each AI mixer change is a card. The card says what changed in plain language ("Keys: −2 dB @ 1.5 kHz, sitting behind vocal"), shows before→after values and a ghost-versus-solid mini curve, and gives an amount slider from −100% (invert) to 150%, plus Undo. The Dugan automix lesson also applies. A muted channel must leave the AI's calculations, because "the AI must respect the human's mute" ([UNVERIFIED, 41k, consistent with Curtis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kHLt2QqNzoU)). Single-press phantom power drew complaints ([V: 119k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dwjwxDjmOBA)), and choosing "PC to Mixer" sync erases the last show ([V: 271k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gTM7IdkCprk)). Destructive actions (preamp gain, routing, scene recall, global mute) therefore need hold-to-confirm, and the AI never performs them. The band's mix-chain spec also says the cloud agent has no write path to the MR18 at all ([V: 157k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y4kxfwblFTs)). The UI should show preamp gain as read-only, with a lock and the label "set at soundcheck."

## Every AI move lands in the sidebar with a reason, a confidence word and an undo

### Anatomy: rail, panel and full log

Kole Jain calls the sidebar "the spine of your product." His advice: icons with short titles that survive collapse, notification badges, a clear active state, and toasts rather than modals for non-blocking notices ([V: 361k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B7k5rOgmOGY)). Time-ordered data reads better as a timeline "tucked into a sidebar pop out" than as a table ([V: 156k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ksx9C2-3yMo)). Collapsed rails need tooltips and fixed item heights so nothing shifts ([V: 102k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FuoP3HS3FB8)). On Dribbble, the icon rail plus expandable panel recurs across five designers ([Dribbble: Louis Nguyen, 89 / 56.4k](https://dribbble.com/shots/25861394-Two-level-Sidebar); [Canva, 120 / 66.3k](https://dribbble.com/shots/5663851-Collapsed-side-bar)). The "Live Activity" row anatomy is name, id·time, and a right-aligned status chip such as "Retry 1/3" in amber or "Tool Error" in red ([Dribbble: Yuri Gokoryan, 133 / 29.3k](https://dribbble.com/shots/27408472-AI-Agent-Observability-Platform-UI-Design)). The collapsible stepper for agent steps comes from the AI Agent Tracker ([Dribbble: Dmitry Sergushkin, 106 / 37.9k](https://dribbble.com/shots/26490547-AI-Agent-Tracker)).

The sidebar sits on the right and never moves the program monitor. Collapsed, it is a **56 px rail**. At the top is the mode badge ("AUTO" in lilac), then one icon per process with a status dot: Director, Mixer, Show flow, Cameras, Encoder/stream, Recording, Server, Learning. Dots are neutral when fine, amber for a warning and red for an error, and a count badge appears only for unresolved errors. At the bottom is the **Take manual control** button as an icon with a tooltip and shortcut. Expanded, it is a **340 px panel** in three blocks, ordered by Geckoboard's "most important... top left-hand corner" rule ([V: 296k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t3cAUt7sOQg)):

1. **Autonomy.** One row per agent (Director, Mixer, Breaks, Go-live/End, Health recovery, Server, Learning). Each defaults to AUTO, with a three-step Manual / Assist / Auto control, and the full-width Take manual control button sits underneath.
2. **Processes.** If everything is healthy this collapses to one line, "All systems OK," as the notes recommend to avoid red/green noise. Otherwise each row shows a dot, a name and a value ("Encoder 6.0 Mbps · 0 dropped").
3. **Activity.** A timeline, newest first. Each row has a time, an agent icon, a one-line verb sentence, a reason chip, a confidence word, and Undo or "Back to previous shot."

Rows expand to show before/after values and the inputs that triggered them (HAX 11A local explanation, 11D input→output mapping, [GUIDELINE](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/library/pattern/)). An "Open full log" link opens the raw model-call trace as a separate route. This is the Copilot pattern: summary plan in view, session log one click away ([GitHub, 75k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1GVBRhDI5No)). Confidence uses words ("sure / likely / guessing"), not "87.3%." HAX pattern 2A says to match the precision of what you show to how well the system actually performs, and stating confidence in words supported calibration better than graphs in the reviewed literature ([GUIDELINE](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2022.883385/epub)). Bursts coalesce ("Mixer: 6 small fader moves, last 10 s") so the feed shows decisions, not ticks (**inference**). The panel remembers its open or closed state per viewer, and never auto-opens during the show.

### Override, locks and undo follow one rule: the human's last touch wins

Mode confusion is the classic automation failure. It occurs when "a machine [is] in a different mode from that anticipated by the user," and pilots report it about twice a year ([GUIDELINE: JATM review](https://jatm.com.br/jatm/article/download/1280/952/6236)). The fixes are a mode badge that is visible even in the collapsed rail, and logging every mode change, including automatic fallbacks, as a feed event (HAX G18, "Notify users about changes"). The override model below applies HAX G9 (correction), G17 (global controls) and 16C (reconfirm), plus the Ecamm and mixer findings above, and is an **inference**:

| Human action | What the AI does | Evidence basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Take manual control** (button or one shortcut) | All agents drop to Manual instantly. The AI keeps observing and logs suggestions. The badge turns grey "MANUAL" and the rail shows a lilac "Resume AI" | HAX G17 [GUIDELINE]; IBM on-the-loop kill switch |
| Resume AI | One confirmation that says what the AI will do next. The AI resumes **from the current human state**, not its old one | HAX 16C [GUIDELINE] |
| Manual cut while Director is AUTO | The cut airs. The Director holds for a dwell window, then resumes its rotation | Ecamm override rule [UNVERIFIED, 22k] |
| Veto during the preview countdown | The proposed cut is cancelled and logged "Vetoed by you". The AI proposes again later | HAX 8 "efficient dismissal" [GUIDELINE] |
| Human moves a fader or knob | That parameter auto-locks for N minutes (lock icon on the strip) | Mixing notes inference; HAX G9 |
| Lock icon on a strip or camera tile | The AI never acts on it, and attempts log as "Skipped: Bass locked by you" | HAX G17; Dugan mute lesson [UNVERIFIED, 41k] |
| Mute | Always wins. The channel leaves the AI's calculations | Dugan post-insert lesson |
| Undo on a mixer card / "Revert AI since…" / snapshot "Manual vs AI" | Values are restored with a short ramp, and the restore is logged as a human action | HAX 9C [GUIDELINE]; Neutron snapshots [V: 162k] |
| "Back to previous shot" on a director card | A new cut to the earlier camera. Aired video cannot be undone | Sidebar notes inference |

### Alerts escalate in four tiers and never block the show

NN/g separates indicators, notifications and validations by urgency and by whether the user must act ([GUIDELINE](https://nngroup.com/articles/indicators-validations-notifications)). Ops tools use three to five severity levels ([Novu](https://novu.co/changelog/notification-severity/); [VMware](https://docs.vmware.com/en/VMware-Telco-Cloud-Operations/1.0/vmware-tco-10-user-guide/GUID-7E0B8687-DFAA-45BD-AA69-5805EF5F403B.html)). Toasts suit warning and error states because they inform without "taking over the entire screen," while modals are reserved for things that must be resolved ([V: 361k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B7k5rOgmOGY)). With your palette, the tiers below map cleanly. The safe-state behaviour follows HAX 10C and 2D ([GUIDELINE](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/library/pattern/)).

| Tier | Examples | Presentation | AI behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Critical** (red) | Stream down, master silent or clipping, encoder dropped, phone on program disconnected | Persistent red banner across the top, visible with the sidebar collapsed. The rail icon pulses. Optional cue in the operator's headphones only | Falls to a safe state (wide shot, last good mix, or break slate), then asks for help |
| **Warning** (amber) | Low battery, weak Wi-Fi, fps under 60, low AI confidence, retry 1/3 | Amber chip on the tile or strip, an amber dot on the rail, and an item pinned at the top of the feed. No popup | Works around it (avoids that camera, holds the mix) and logs it |
| **Info** (neutral) | Normal cuts, fader moves, break started | Feed only, fade-in with no slide | Acts |
| **Debug** | Model calls, parameter traces | Full log route only | — |

Never rely on colour alone. Every status pairs colour with an icon and a word, and a **Focus** toggle suppresses info-level badges during songs. Only material state transitions should announce through ARIA live regions ([UNVERIFIED, single vendor](https://gravitee.io/corpus/gen-2232/bootstrap-front-end-framework/ai-agent-status-badges.html)).

## Blue means on air, red means broken, and the sunrise brand never touches status

The Dribbble notes proposed a warm near-black base with a red program tally. Your decisions replace the red tally with blue and confine red to errors. The warm base is still well supported. The Hyper streaming shot's palette is terracotta #BF4C2F and copper #C78463 on #050506 ([Dribbble: Erik Padamans, 301 / 68.9k](https://dribbble.com/shots/26554251-Hyper-Desktop-Vtube-App-Streaming-UI)). The Sentry camera UI uses #0D0D0B and #453A30 ([Dribbble: Phenomenon, 191 / 38.8k](https://dribbble.com/shots/26952820-Live-Camera-Monitoring-Mobile-UI-Sentry)). A single saturated accent on black carries the whole identity of the Rtt-01 plugin ([Dribbble: Riotters, 344 / 79.7k](https://dribbble.com/shots/24463314-Rtt-01-music-plugin)). The pink→orange gradient disc in Fusion 002 is directly on-brand ([Dribbble: Julian Behrens, 111 / 17.2k](https://dribbble.com/shots/24845241-Multiband-Saturator-VST-Plugin-GUI)).

Two consequences of your decisions shape the palette (**inference**). First, the notes suggested blue for "AI engaged," after Tesla's blue Autosteer icon ([UNVERIFIED, forum](https://www.teslamotorsclub.com/tmc/threads/small-steaming-wheel-icon-upper-left-side-of-interface.131138/)). Blue now means on air, so the AI gets its own hue: a **dawn lilac**, the pre-sunrise sky. It is far from every semantic colour. Second, the brand sunrise orange and the warning amber have nearly the same luminance (1.30:1 against each other), so they can only be told apart by hue. The rule is therefore strict: **brand orange is never used for state**. It appears only on the logo, the break and starting-soon slates, the master strip's gradient and focus rings on brand surfaces. ## Conclusion

The research reframes the brief. "As simple as the easiest streaming software" is not mainly a question of fewer controls. In an AI-run show the human's job is *supervision*, and the evidence on supervision is about status, explanation and reversibility, not layout. That makes the preview slot and the sidebar the product's core. The preview becomes the AI's stated intention with a veto window, and the sidebar becomes a readable diary of decisions with undo. The mixing station's depth then costs nothing in simplicity, because it only appears when a human reaches past the AI. Your blue on-air decision turned out to solve a problem the notes could not solve cleanly: red meaning both "live" and "broken." The palette's real remaining risk is the brand orange drifting into status use next to amber, and that needs enforcing in code review.

Three things remain unproven and should be tested in the first shows rather than assumed. No product shows an AI director's next shot and reasons live, so the length of the veto countdown and how readable the reasons are need rehearsal data. No guideline gives numbers for undo windows, auto-lock duration after a human fader move, or alert rate limits for live media. Phone battery and thermal alerts are an inferred need with no observed precedent. Log every override during rehearsals: each "Take manual control" press, veto and lock is direct evidence of where the AI's AUTO default is not yet trusted.
