## Q1. Which mixer UIs do engineers praise as fastest/clearest, and why? What are the common complaints?

### Takeaway
Mixing Station is the most-praised remote UI. Reviewers credit three things:
- one consistent layout across every console brand;
- layers and channel strips the user fully customizes, showing only the channels in use and resizing to fit;
- a reliable "sends on fader" mode with clear mode coloring.

Praise across products comes down to "only what I need, on one screen, big targets." The complaints are mostly:
- control lag;
- small or cramped screens;
- too many ways to do the same thing;
- hidden or temporary navigation;
- unreliable Wi-Fi.

### Cited Findings
**Mixing Station (third-party app)**
- Called "the best app that I've used to mix digital mixers." It runs on phones as well as tablets, uses "the same layout in the same app no matter which mixer," and has a reputation for crashing less than the vendor apps. [V: 353k; A: 2 channels] — [Scott Uhl](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bGcOnbUFjA0); [Vito Sound](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iGphpo6D8Pg) ("incredibly reliable and fully customizable… learn the workflow on one mixer… you don't have to relearn")
- Layers are user-defined:
  - You set "channels per layer" (6, 8, 16, …). With 6 inputs, you set layers to 6 and get "nice big faders."
  - Unused buses, FX and DCAs can be hidden from the sidebar.
  - Strips can be reordered (for example, putting the main fader on the same page as the channels) and spacers inserted.
  - A "fit channels" option resizes faders to fill the screen as items are added or removed.
  [V: 353k; A: 2 channels] — [Scott Uhl](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bGcOnbUFjA0); [Vito Sound](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iGphpo6D8Pg)
- The channel strip itself is configurable: show or hide the icon, compressor threshold, compressor graph, gain knob and solo, and reorder and resize each element (for example, a bigger mute or pan). Gestures are configurable too: double-tap resets a fader to unity, long-press opens a fine-adjust popup for pan. [V: 353k] — [Scott Uhl](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bGcOnbUFjA0)
- There is a "Fine" fader mode, because the default fader moves "very quickly… drastic." [V: 353k] — [Scott Uhl](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bGcOnbUFjA0)
- Two-tier channel view:
  - Tapping a channel name opens an overview page with gain, phase, phantom and stereo, plus mini gate, EQ and compressor graphs and all sends. Tapping any graph opens its full editor.
  - Top tabs lead to deeper pages.
  - Left and right arrows step to the previous or next channel.
  [V: 353k; A: 2 channels] — [Scott Uhl](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bGcOnbUFjA0); [Vito Sound](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iGphpo6D8Pg)
- A button with "three dots" means it has a long-press action. This is a visible affordance for hidden gestures. [UNVERIFIED-ish: 82k, 1 channel; but the Ui24R uses the same pattern, see Q2] — [Vito Sound](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iGphpo6D8Pg)
- Custom layouts: a blank-canvas editor where you place faders, knobs and buttons, then bind each one to a "current channel" or a "fixed channel" action (for example, "C3 mute" or "C3 pan"). [V: 353k] — [Scott Uhl](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bGcOnbUFjA0)

**Common complaints**
- Yamaha DM7: buttons and paging lag before the faders move; "there's 99 ways to do the same thing"; the UI pushes the touchscreen over knobs; the bus bank selector "disappears after a few seconds." [UNVERIFIED: 30k, 1 channel] — [ByronBelleMusic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tqWTsCC9s78)
- Yamaha DM3: the touchscreen is "tiny… squinting," "small and so glitchy." The reviewer returned it for a WING Compact with a 10" tilting screen and user-customizable layers. [UNVERIFIED: 15k, 1 channel] — [drPeks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5CTAwKz0Ca4)
- On the other side, Sweetwater calls the DM3 "easy to use and navigate," with an always-present Home button, both touch and the touch-and-turn knob for every parameter, and scene presets (rock band, jazz combo…) "to get you in the ballpark." [V: 279k] — [Sweetwater](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s5T9P-Cx8Oc). Scott Uhl agrees on the Home button and scene templates. [A: 2 channels] — [Scott Uhl DM3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dwjwxDjmOBA)
- DM3 phantom power toggles on a single press. The reviewer wished it were press-and-hold to avoid damaging gear by accident (the console has a global 48V-disable setting). [V: 119k] — [Scott Uhl DM3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dwjwxDjmOBA)
- XR18/MR18 built-in Wi-Fi: "there are two kinds of XR18 users: those whose Wi-Fi has failed them at a gig and those that will." Use an external router. [V: 271k] — [Alan Hamilton](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gTM7IdkCprk)
- XR18/X-Air Edit sync trap: "Mixer to PC" versus "PC to Mixer" sync. Choosing PC to Mixer erases the last show on the mixer. [V: 271k] — [Alan Hamilton](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gTM7IdkCprk)
- X-Air Edit: bus buttons do not show bus names until you enable a GUI preference ("show bus names"). The reviewer names this as "probably the one change you'll want to make." [V: 271k] — [Alan Hamilton](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gTM7IdkCprk)
- Tap points (pre/post EQ, pre/post fader) confuse users. Alan Hamilton explains them in both X-Air Edit and Mixing Station: tap points sit in a per-send drop-down, and a "globe" icon applies a tap-point change to all channels. [V: 271k + 105k, 1 channel] — [Alan Hamilton](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gTM7IdkCprk); [Alan Hamilton](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hUxVALTQ3Po)
- Ui24R: a skeptic's worry is that "you can only really see one channel at a time" versus an analog console. The answer offered is a full-mixer overview plus a quick edit page. [V: 158k] — [Andertons](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uThRC8GMmD8)
- Waves LV1: "pretty much everybody who touches LV1, even from the studio world, within 5 minutes" is comfortable. Users can tear off tabs into separate windows, and a fader stays latched to the finger that grabbed it until release. [UNVERIFIED: 70k, 1 channel] — [Sweetwater LV1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AexpXnwXMcY)
- UA LUNA:
  - Praised for a clean, uncluttered layout.
  - An "all tracks" group is created by default.
  - Global bypass buttons for extensions, inserts and "all processing" let you A/B the whole mix.
  - A global clip indicator shows "something is clipped in the session."
  - Mix versions can be saved and compared.
  [UNVERIFIED: 81k, 1 channel] — [Mixing Lessons](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vgyr58KLYBE)

### Inferences
- The winning pattern is "show only what this show uses." For a 16-channel MR18 band, default to one layer with all used inputs, the main fader and the IEM bus masters, sized to fit. No paging.
- Dangerous actions (phantom power, scene recall, push-to-mixer sync, global mute) should require hold-to-confirm. AI-initiated changes should never perform them.
- Low-latency feedback is non-negotiable. Lag is the top complaint about even a flagship console. Over a cloud round trip, the web app needs optimistic local UI with confirmed state shown separately.

### Gaps
- I found no readable transcripts for X32-Edit, M-Air Edit (other than the automix knowledge-base video title), DiGiCo, dLive or SQ MixPad (only titles and channel listings), or Yamaha StageMix. I could not verify engineer praise or complaints for these from YouTube.
- The WING official overview (142k) had no captions. Customizable WING layers are supported only by low-view or single-channel sources: drPeks, plus WING how-to titles from the Behringer Knowledge Base (22k) and Drew Brashler (20k).

## Q2. What are the best web and tablet mixer UI patterns? (The Ui24R is browser-based.)

### Takeaway
The Ui24R shows that a browser-only mixer is credible:
- any HTML5 device works with no install;
- many devices connect at once, each independent;
- per-musician "MORE ME" monitor control is built in.

Common tablet patterns across Ui24R, Mixing Station and UC Surface:
- big draggable graph handles (EQ dots, threshold lines);
- touch-and-hold indicated by a corner glyph;
- double-tap to reset;
- an overview page with mini graphs that drill down to full editors;
- sends-on-fader with a tinted background;
- role-based device permissions.

### Cited Findings
- The Ui24R is controlled from a web browser. "Every device is a web browser," so there is no app download and no iOS/Android split. Several devices connect simultaneously and independently, and changes reflect on all of them. It was described as "very lightweight" and "astoundingly stable." [V: 158k; A: 2 channels] — [Andertons](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uThRC8GMmD8); [Mix-Record-Connect](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=np5pDM1a5eQ) ("any HTML5 compliant device"; 185k)
- Ui24R hidden-action affordance: any button with a small mark in its top-right corner supports touch-and-hold, for example click-and-hold on the channel name tab for presets, rename, copy, subgroup/VCA assignment, stereo link, reset and "assign me." [V: 185k] — [Mix-Record-Connect](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=np5pDM1a5eQ). The same convention appears in Mixing Station ("three dots"). [A: 2 channels] — [Vito Sound](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iGphpo6D8Pg)
- Ui24R fader color encodes state: a fader turns green instead of blue when the channel is assigned to a VCA. Double-click a value to reset it to 0 dB; click and hold to type an exact value. [V: 185k] — [Mix-Record-Connect](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=np5pDM1a5eQ)
- Ui24R mini strip on the mixer view: each strip shows a compressor/gate indicator, a VU meter (input and output), mute, solo, level and pan. Double-clicking the fader cap opens the channel editor. [V: 185k + 158k; A: 2 channels] — [Mix-Record-Connect](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=np5pDM1a5eQ); [Andertons](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uThRC8GMmD8)
- Ui24R EQ editor:
  - HPF/LPF with slope choice, 4-band PEQ with values in a side panel.
  - A built-in RTA, plus a "DeEsser-style" grab-the-peak-and-pull-it-down interaction (the caption reads "DSA").
  - An "Easy EQ" mode collapses the EQ to bass/mid/treble.
  - The gate and compressor show big draggable threshold displays with the advanced controls (attack, hold, release) beneath.
  [V: 185k + 158k; A: 2 channels] — [Mix-Record-Connect](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=np5pDM1a5eQ); [Andertons](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uThRC8GMmD8)
- Ui24R RTA on aux outputs to spot feedback: select an aux, press RTA, and a "spike popping up" shows the feeding-back frequency so you can pull it down on the graphic EQ. The unit also has AFS2 automatic feedback suppression on every aux output. [V: 158k] — [Andertons](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uThRC8GMmD8)
- XR18/X-Air Edit also offers the RTA inside the channel EQ section: "it will show us exactly where feedback is happening." [V: 271k; A: 2 channels with Ui24R] — [Alan Hamilton](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gTM7IdkCprk)
- EQ touch gestures in Mixing Station: drag the colored band dots, and pinch to change Q. Graphs show live signal behind the dynamics curve. [UNVERIFIED-ish: 82k] — [Vito Sound](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iGphpo6D8Pg). The DM3 also lets you "grab [the EQ] and move it around" or use the encoder. [V: 279k; A: 2 channels] — [Sweetwater DM3S](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s5T9P-Cx8Oc)
- Sends on fader:
  - Selecting a bus (for example, a musician's IEM mix) makes the channel faders show the send levels to that bus.
  - Mixing Station can tint the whole background in the bus's color (pink for one musician, blue for another, red for the drummer). The reviewer calls this "a good way to not accidentally forget… I'm on my ears when I'm trying to adjust front of house."
  - A "large" setup shows all bus buttons; a "small" one uses a picker.
  [V: 353k; A: 2 channels] — [Scott Uhl](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bGcOnbUFjA0); [Vito Sound](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iGphpo6D8Pg); WING Edit also offers sends on fader: [Alan Hamilton WING](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eDTr0d6LGU4) (116k)
- Ui24R aux mixing: "simply by pressing aux… selecting aux 1… now I'm mixing into aux 1… always a button press away." [V: 158k] — [Andertons](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uThRC8GMmD8)
- Role-scoped access:
  - Mixing Station offers "No restrictions" or "Personal mixer" (pick a bus, and the musician controls only their IEM mix), plus password-protected access. [V: 353k; A: 2 channels] — [Scott Uhl](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bGcOnbUFjA0); [Vito Sound](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iGphpo6D8Pg)
  - PreSonus UC Surface gives per-device permissions (FOH, a specific aux, or all auxes) and QMix-UC for musicians' phones, "preventing them from accidentally adjusting someone else's mix." [UNVERIFIED-ish: 76k, but it agrees with the 2 above → A: 3 channels] — [Fender Studio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZCkg2IYzF4)
  - Ui24R "MORE ME" / "assign me" works the same way. [V: 185k] — [Mix-Record-Connect](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=np5pDM1a5eQ)
- Theming for venues: Mixing Station ships high-contrast and other themes for "playing outside in really bright environments where dark mode isn't really working," and a full theme editor. [V: 353k] — [Scott Uhl](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bGcOnbUFjA0)
- Naming, colors and icons:
  - Long-press a channel name to rename it, color it and pick an icon (Mixing Station). [V: 353k; A: 2 channels] — [Scott Uhl](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bGcOnbUFjA0); [Vito Sound](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iGphpo6D8Pg)
  - The DM3 has an icon picker plus name suggestions per icon ("vocal," "MC"), and its users color-code by category (all vocals red). [V: 119k] — [Scott Uhl DM3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dwjwxDjmOBA)
  - Label by source or stage position, not musician names (for handoff to venue techs). [V: 116k] — [Alan Hamilton WING](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eDTr0d6LGU4)
- DCAs and groups:
  - DCAs control a set of channel levels without routing audio. Subgroups route audio, so you can EQ them.
  - Ui24R colors faders to show DCA membership.
  - Mixing Station allows ad-hoc "IDCA" strips (named, colored, for example a magenta "keys" DCA on channels 12–15) that can be placed anywhere in a layer.
  [V: 158k; A: 2 channels] — [Andertons](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uThRC8GMmD8); [Vito Sound](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iGphpo6D8Pg)
- Mute groups: Mixing Station has a "mute enable" safety toggle that must be on before channel mutes respond, but mute groups (for example, "band") still work when it is off. [A: 2 channels; V: 353k] — [Scott Uhl](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bGcOnbUFjA0); [Vito Sound](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iGphpo6D8Pg)
- Scenes and snapshots:
  - Mixing Station: long-press a slot to save, then choose what to include or exclude (for example, global settings). Named snapshots per lineup ("Acoustic solo").
  - Scenes can be backed up to the tablet and ported across mixer families. Mixing Station also exposes the console's scene "safes."
  [V: 353k; A: 2 channels] — [Scott Uhl](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bGcOnbUFjA0); [Vito Sound](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iGphpo6D8Pg)
  - The DM3 ships starter scene templates (rock band, jazz combo, video meeting, karaoke) and auto-remembers state. [V: 279k; A: 2 channels] — [Sweetwater](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s5T9P-Cx8Oc); [Scott Uhl DM3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dwjwxDjmOBA)
- Gain staging guidance shown in the UI: digital meters read dBFS. Aim for about −18 dBFS average with peaks up to 6 dB higher. Gain is first in the chain, so changing it changes every monitor send. [V: 271k] — [Alan Hamilton](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gTM7IdkCprk). The DM3 gain page shows a green target zone on a graph ("when it's on that green spot, that's usually a good spot"). [V: 119k] — [Scott Uhl DM3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dwjwxDjmOBA)
- The Mixing Station meters page covers inputs, FX, mixes and DCAs, with configurable metering and averaging. [UNVERIFIED: 82k] — [Vito Sound](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iGphpo6D8Pg)
- Mixing Station has a screen-orientation lock for walking around a venue with a tablet. [UNVERIFIED: 82k] — [Vito Sound](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iGphpo6D8Pg)

### Inferences
Recommended UI for the Sunrise Jam app:
1. **Main "Mix" view.** One fit-to-width layer showing:
   - the 16 inputs, named, colored and with icons by section (rhythm, horns, keys, vox);
   - FX returns;
   - IEM bus masters;
   - the main LR fader;
   - DCA strips (Drums, Horns, Vox, Band).
   Each strip carries a compact gain-reduction bar, a peak/RMS meter with a −18 dBFS target tick, M/S buttons and an "AI" badge.
2. **Sends on fader.** Tap a bus chip to flip the faders to send levels and tint the background in that bus's color.
3. **Quick channel view.** Tapping a strip opens a drawer with mini HPF/gate/EQ/comp graphs, sends and gain. Tapping a mini graph opens the full editor (EQ with RTA overlay, pinch for Q). Prev/next arrows step channels.
4. **Gestures.** Double-tap resets, long-press opens fine/typed entry, and a visible corner glyph marks hold actions.
5. **Roles.** FOH, musician (single IEM bus) and viewer roles, as in UC Surface, Mixing Station and Ui24R.
6. **Themes.** High-contrast and outdoor themes for a sunrise show.

### Gaps
- None of the videos showed LUFS metering in these mixer UIs. Loudness metering for streams appears to live in external plugins and meters, not the console remotes reviewed. A dedicated search would be needed.
- There was little detail on how gain-reduction meters are drawn in Mixing Station or X-Air Edit beyond "threshold" or "comp graph" strip options.

## Q3. EQ and dynamics display benchmark (FabFilter Pro-Q 4, Smart:EQ 4, consoles)

### Takeaway
Pro-Q is the reference because of its UI. Three things stand out:
- a direct-manipulation curve (double-click shows a preview of the band about to be created);
- dynamic and spectral modes that live inside the same band;
- multi-instance views that overlay other tracks' EQs and flag frequency collisions.

### Cited Findings
- "User interface is important and it doesn't mean that it has to be aesthetically good, it has to be good for your workflow." FabFilter has been good at this "since the first Pro-Q." Pro-Q 4 now previews which band type a double-click will create ("less confusing"). [V: 127k] — [White Sea Studio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tejnPHku378)
- Pro-Q 4 instance list:
  - Shows other tracks' EQ curves in one window.
  - Allows pinning channels and adjusting their output levels ("kind of mix in here").
  - Has a frequency "collisions" view.
  - The reviewer asked for per-track color tinting so it is clear which instance is bass or drums, and for solo linking.
  [V: 127k] — [White Sea Studio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tejnPHku378)
- Pro-Q 4 dynamic EQ:
  - The threshold is automatic when collapsed, with manual override.
  - Bands can sidechain from another band.
  - There is a spectral mode per band.
  - Clean/subtle/warm character options.
  [V: 127k; A: 2 channels] — [White Sea Studio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tejnPHku378); [Streaky](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mgWGnRFI_wg) (72k: "standard for big shifts, dynamic for movement, spectral for subtle… you've literally got it all covered")
- Streaky's demo always ends with a level-matched "before and after." [UNVERIFIED single: 72k, but before/after A/B also appears in Q4 sources]
- Dynamics on tablet: the Ui24R gate and compressor give a "nice big display, you can just grab the threshold," which is "a really, really nice way of working" on a tablet. [V: 158k] — [Andertons](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uThRC8GMmD8). Mixing Station likewise lets you grab circles on the dynamics graph, with live signal shown on the grid. [A: 2 channels] — [Vito Sound](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iGphpo6D8Pg)
- Compressor "mix" (parallel) control exists on XR18/MR18 channels: 100% means normal, 0% means no compression. [V: 271k] — [Alan Hamilton](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gTM7IdkCprk)

### Inferences
- The full EQ editor should copy Pro-Q:
  - direct-drag nodes with a band-type preview;
  - a pre/post RTA overlay;
  - optional ghost curves of competing channels (for example, bass versus kick) with collision highlights.
- Color-tint each EQ and dynamics editor in the channel's color, as the reviewer requested.
- MR18 limits (4-band PEQ, gate, comp with mix) should be shown honestly. Don't fake dynamic EQ the hardware can't do.

### Gaps
- No video showed Pro-Q-class interaction on the MR18's own editors. Whether the MR18's OSC update rate supports smooth RTA in a cloud web app is outside YouTube scope.

## Q4. How should AI-made changes be presented on a mixer (diff, undo, lock, confidence)? Covers automix UIs.

### Takeaway
Every AI tool reviewers liked keeps three things in place:
1. it shows its proposed curve or balance as a visible, editable object;
2. it lets you scale it ("agree/disagree" amount, including inverting it);
3. it makes level-matched before/after A/B trivial.

Reviewers consistently treat AI output as a "starting point" to tweak, never a black box. Dugan-style automix UIs show the per-channel gain each mic is currently receiving, with weight and group controls.

### Cited Findings
**Sonible smart:EQ 4: the scale/invert pattern**
- The "smart band" shows the AI's suggested curve. You can make it "more extreme, less extreme, inverted (so disagree with it)," limit it to a frequency range, or split it into two smart bands to "disagree with one part of the spectrum and agree with the other." The reviewer: "when there is a result… 'No, I actually meant the opposite.' You just quickly drag it the opposite way… it just works." [V: 102k] — [White Sea Studio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I2FHV3li314)
- smart:EQ 4 learning options: configurable maximum learning time (the reviewer complains AI often "has its setting before it actually has heard everything") and "auto learn on startup."
- Profiles: once learned, you can switch profile without relearning.
- Smoothing control, and auto-gain so soloing and EQing doesn't change the balance.
[V: 102k] — [White Sea Studio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I2FHV3li314)
- smart:EQ 4 group view:
  - Tracks are dragged into a front/back hierarchy, and the AI recomputes each curve ("swap these… drums more in the background, and you can see how the curve changes").
  - It controls every group member's EQ from one screen, "which is also what I really like from mixing on an analog console."
  - A mode knob chooses Track / Group / Track+Group.
  [V: 102k] — [White Sea Studio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I2FHV3li314)
- Real-world use: a dozen smart:EQs set to learn produced "a workable sound… in a few minutes" versus about 2 hours. Then "I can basically just jump into the EQs and adjust whatever I want." [V: 102k] — [White Sea Studio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I2FHV3li314)

**iZotope Neutron 5 (Mix Assistant, Visual Mixer)**
- The UI flow: "learning track sonic profile → searching for enhancement." It shows the detected instrument (kick, snare…), which the user can correct when it misidentifies (it "didn't identify the snare as a snare"). The reviewer had to reclassify a source to get the right sliders: "it's very important to get the right preset." [V: 162k] — [White Sea Studio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zOxo81pijoU)
- Visual Mixer:
  - All channels are placed as objects on a 2D field (vertical = louder or quieter, horizontal = pan), which the reviewer called "not a bad way."
  - The reviewer missed a solo function on it.
  - Afterwards he removed it to "dig into all the settings… check what is going on."
  - Snapshots of the "manual balance" versus "auto balance" mixes were compared, and he preferred the auto one ("way more focused").
  [V: 162k] — [White Sea Studio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zOxo81pijoU); topic popularity: [iZotope Visual Mixer intro](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_FLRDh-fZKg) (158k)
- Criticisms of Neutron 5:
  - It "keeps trying to use the same chain… doesn't really dare to do something different."
  - It is CPU-heavy.
  - Some AI choices were wrong (for example, upward compression on a kick).
  - Its full processing is exposed, so each module can be audited.
  [V: 162k] — [White Sea Studio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zOxo81pijoU)

**Waves AQ and SSL auto plugins: "starting point" consensus**
- Waves AQ:
  - Shows the detected source (kick, snare, female vocal) and lets the user switch between alternative suggested curves ("brighter kick… fatter").
  - Before/after comparison is central.
  - The reviewer: "there's still a space for someone who knows what they're doing… if I had time to tweak every one, would I still do it? Yeah."
  - Live versions run on LV1/SuperRack.
  [UNVERIFIED alone: 48k] — [Churchfront](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WrdGPGChsng)
- SSL auto EQ and dynamics:
  - The reviewer reads out exactly what the AI did ("rolled off ~2 dB at 30 Hz… +5 dB at 200 Hz… −5 dB at 1.5k") from the visible curve.
  - He calls results "hit or miss": "you can learn it and then you've got to go in and tweak it."
  - He had to level-match manually because the auto compressor didn't lower output, and he flagged an 8:1 ratio as suspicious.
  [UNVERIFIED alone: 78k] — [Home Recording Made Easy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IeAV7nmZXVs)
- Agreement across White Sea Studio (162k/102k), Churchfront (48k) and Home Recording Made Easy (78k): AI gives a fast starting point, the change must be visible and readable, and the human must tweak and A/B with level matching. [A: 3 channels; V: 162k]

**Automix (Dugan) display**
- A Dugan is gain-sharing, not a gate. The display shows per-channel bar meters of the gain each mic currently receives. Each channel has a "weight" control to bias a weak mic (for example, a lav placed low). Channels are grouped (A, B…) and groups are color-coded on the DM7. [A: 3 channels: KINETIC (41k), Michael Curtis (15k), ByronBelleMusic (30k)] — [KINETIC](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kHLt2QqNzoU); [Michael Curtis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4eGSl0T2N1Q); [ByronBelleMusic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tqWTsCC9s78)
- Key automix pitfall:
  - With the insert set pre-on/off, a muted channel (for example, a presenter being miked backstage) still steals gain.
  - The fix is a post-on insert, so the channel mute removes the channel from the automix.
  - Lesson: the AI must respect the human's mute.
  [UNVERIFIED alone: 41k, but consistent with Curtis's "it's not mixing for us… know when to give it help"] — [KINETIC](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kHLt2QqNzoU)
- "Auto mix is not going to mix your band." It is for panels and speech. You still set gain, EQ and fader, then assign mics to automix group X or Y; "weight at zero is typically fine." This applies to the XR18/MR18 itself. [V: 271k] — [Alan Hamilton](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gTM7IdkCprk)
- The DM7 offers 64 Dugan instances with a "Dugan" button in the channel home that opens an overview; changing group "changes color." [UNVERIFIED: 30k] — [ByronBelleMusic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tqWTsCC9s78)

### Inferences
Recommended UI for Claude's actions on the MR18:
1. **Change feed (diff).** A collapsible sidebar "Processes" timeline. Each AI action is a card in plain language, for example: "Ch5 Rhodes: −2.5 dB @ 320 Hz (mud), +1 dB fader; confidence 0.72; reason: masking with Bass." Each card shows a before→after value pair and a mini-curve diff (ghost old curve, solid new curve), copying how reviewers read SSL/AQ curves.
2. **Amount slider per suggestion,** from −100% (invert) through 0 to 100% to 150%, copying Sonible's smart band. Apply partially rather than all-or-nothing.
3. **Lock/pin per channel or parameter.** The AI may not touch a parameter the human has locked. A human fader move auto-locks that parameter for N minutes, and channel mute always wins (the Dugan post-insert lesson).
4. **Undo/A-B.** Per-card undo, a "revert all AI changes since scene X," and a level-matched A/B toggle (auto-gain, as Sonible does). Snapshot "Manual vs AI" mixes, as in Neutron.
5. **Strip badge.** An "AI" dot on any strip the AI touched in the last N seconds, tinted by confidence. Show the detected instrument class and allow one-tap correction (Neutron misclassifications).
6. **Automix-style live view.** For any gain-riding Claude does (for example, solos or horn features), show Dugan-style bars of the gain offset applied per channel, plus per-channel weight and include/exclude toggles.
7. **Safety rails.** The AI never touches phantom power, routing or scene recall without hold-to-confirm, and limits the rate of change (small steps, smoothing).

### Gaps
- I found no YouTube review of an AI live-console mixing assistant that shows an explicit confidence score or a change-log/diff UI. Confidence display is my inference, not a pattern documented by reviewers.
- RoEx Automix (127k promo, vendor channel) and Moises were found but not reviewed. Vendor promos don't meet the independence bar.

## Simple vs advanced modes (cross-cutting)

### Takeaway
Products handle simple versus advanced in four ways:
- (a) Easy EQ / single-knob modes alongside the full editor (Ui24R Easy EQ, DM3 one-knob compressor);
- (b) a setup wizard that sets screen density;
- (c) hiding unused buses and strips;
- (d) role-restricted "personal mixer" modes.

### Cited Findings
- Ui24R "Easy EQ" collapses the EQ to bass/mid/treble. [V: 185k] — [Mix-Record-Connect](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=np5pDM1a5eQ)
- The DM3 has "smart features like a single-knob compression." [V: 119k] — [Scott Uhl DM3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dwjwxDjmOBA)
- The Mixing Station setup wizard asks channels-per-screen and strip contents on first run, and is re-runnable. Reset-to-default for layers acts as an escape hatch: "if you screwed up… just hit yes… go back to normal." [V: 353k; A: 2 channels] — [Scott Uhl](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bGcOnbUFjA0); [Vito Sound](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iGphpo6D8Pg)
- Neutron 5 offers "simplified controls" on modules, with full modules underneath. [V: 162k] — [White Sea Studio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zOxo81pijoU)

### Inferences
- Default to Simple mode: faders, mutes, meters, IEM chips, the AI feed and macro knobs (for example, "Vocal presence," "Low-end tightness"). Advanced mode reveals the full gate/EQ/comp editors per channel.
- Keep a one-tap "reset layout" escape hatch.
