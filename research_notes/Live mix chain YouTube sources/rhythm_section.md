## 1. Kick and snare chains for funk (tight, dry, ghost notes audible): gate vs expander, compression numbers

### Takeaway
High-view sources agree on four things. Put a partial-range gate or expander on the kick, not a hard gate (about 12–20 dB range, very fast attack, short hold, fast release). Do **not** gate the snare, because ghost notes get lost. Kick compression: 4–5:1, attack about 15–36 ms, release about 60–120 ms, 2–6 dB GR. Snare compression: about 4:1 with a 15 ms attack and 50 ms release. This lifts ghost notes and rolls relative to the backbeat. A slower 50 ms attack with heavier GR is an alternative for more crack and length. Snare EQ: HPF about 100–120 Hz, cut boxiness about 400–460 Hz or 600–900 Hz, boost about 2.5 kHz and about 8 kHz.

### Cited Findings
**Kick gate / expander**
- Kick gate: attack "as fast as I can get it", hold about 45 ms ("you just need that first 40 to 50 possibly 60 milliseconds"), release 10 ms, **range 12 dB** ("don't go all the way off"). The key HPF is about 200 Hz so the gate triggers on the beater slap, not resonance. On Behringer/Midas the gate key is taken pre-EQ. The engineer says: "I gate the kick, I do not gate the snare, I do gate the toms (~10 dB range)." — [A](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H9NbaELL6nE) **[VERIFIED: 227k]**
- Partial range rather than a full gate is supported by a second channel. Jake Russell on X32 sets kick gate range "to about 20 [dB] to make it sound a little more natural", with the key filter at 60 Hz. — [U1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EN86Ct4YTFw). Ross Stratton uses an **expander** rather than a gate on kick and subkick ("doesn't close hard… softly fades in and fades out") to suppress bass-guitar bleed. — [E](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FIlwKWlfXc0) **[3 channels; 227k+91k+2.5k]**
- Key-filter frequency **conflicts**: about 200 Hz HPF on the key per [A](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H9NbaELL6nE), versus 60 Hz per [U1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EN86Ct4YTFw).

**Snare gate**
- No gate on the snare, to protect ghost notes: "there is no gate on either the top or bottom snare mic… you kind of have ghost notes… you might miss some intricate parts" — [E](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FIlwKWlfXc0). "I do not gate the snare." — [A](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H9NbaELL6nE). A also warns that gates "eliminate a performance" when the drummer plays soft. **[VERIFIED: 2 channels; 227k+91k]**

**Kick compression**
- Kick in: 5:1, attack about 30–36 ms ("transients on kick drums are somewhere between 30 and 50 ms"), hold 0, release 70–80 ms, 2–3 dB GR ("more than enough especially in the live arena"). Kick out: 5:1, attack about 24–25 ms, faster release. — [B](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WxOnpsuAsqw) **[VERIFIED: 215k]**
- Corroborating channels: 4:1, attack 15 ms, release 59 ms, makeup 3 dB, GR 3–6 dB on X32 — [U1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EN86Ct4YTFw). 2.5:1, attack 30 ms ("lets the point of the kick breathe"), release 116 ms, about 2–4 dB GR — [U2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oDbyvsEjdg4). 4:1 peak mode with a hard knee; the kick attack can be slower than the snare's 15 ms, and about 7 ms brings out beater click — [C](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CshdvNnDKr4). 7:1, "mildly fast attack, mildly slow release" — [E](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FIlwKWlfXc0). **[VERIFIED: 5 channels; 215k+145k+91k+10k+2.5k]**

**Kick EQ**
- HPF about 38 Hz; scoop about 285 Hz; wide cut near 2 kHz; high band boost at 16–17 kHz for click; low band boost for thud. — [D](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SY-BnLQNwOc) **[VERIFIED: 222k]**
- Scheps "sucks out" about 118 Hz on the kick to leave room for the bass. — [K](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BMW4K7FzQp8) **[VERIFIED: 219k]**
- Ross Stratton (beater mic, with a separate subkick providing lows): dip at 150 Hz, boost at 1.3 kHz for attack, high shelf at 8.5 kHz. — [E](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FIlwKWlfXc0). A low-mid scoop at 150–285 Hz plus a click boost agrees with [D](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SY-BnLQNwOc). **[2 channels; 222k+91k]**. Note that E's 80 Hz HPF applies only because a subkick covers the lows. Sunrise Jam's rule forbids high-cutting kick lows, and with a single kick mic the HPF should stay near 30–40 Hz, as in D.

**Snare compression**
- 4:1, peak mode, attack 15 ms, release 50 ms. Compression "changed the balance between the cadence rolls and the hits, brought the cadence rolls up," meaning ghost notes become more audible. Attack 15→7→3 ms gets progressively thinner and snappier. "I typically won't go beyond 15 ms for the snare." — [C](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CshdvNnDKr4) **[VERIFIED: 145k]**
- Alternative "explosive" setting for snare top: 7:1, attack 50 ms, hold 0, release about 90 ms (then shortened), heavy GR. B warns that compression raises cymbal bleed and the noise floor. Snare bottom: attack 0, heavy GR, slow release to lengthen rattle. — [B](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WxOnpsuAsqw) **[VERIFIED: 215k]**. Ross Stratton agrees in direction: lower ratio than the kick and slower attack "so it gets that crack… then tightens right up" — [E](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FIlwKWlfXc0).

**Snare EQ**
- Boost 8 kHz (crack; limited by hi-hat bleed), boost 200 Hz (fatness), boost about 2.5 kHz ("translates on a lot of systems… small speakers"), cut 600–900 Hz if honky. Then EQ feeds the compressor. — [F](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yCC3MYf0-yQ) **[VERIFIED: 387k]**
- HPF about 100–120 Hz: 120 Hz per [D](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SY-BnLQNwOc) and 100 Hz per [E](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FIlwKWlfXc0). **[VERIFIED: 2 channels; 222k+91k]**
- Boxiness cut about 415–460 Hz: about 415–417 Hz per [D](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SY-BnLQNwOc) and 460 Hz per [E](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FIlwKWlfXc0). **[VERIFIED: 2 channels]**. Conflict on 200 Hz: F boosts it for fatness, while E dips about 190–200 Hz ("a little too much body"). Treat this as drum-dependent.

**Neo-soul (Voodoo) character**
- Drums mono and close-miked (little room); overhead "almost entirely uncompressed" so hi-hat/snare balance does not pump; dynamics controlled with **saturation** instead. Snare: 1073 brightening plus 1176, stylized "mid-range heavy", then compression plus **limiting** to flatten the attack, and saturation "to get some length". Kick: two-band multiband expander (about 50–100 Hz and 100–175 Hz bands lowered, then opened on each hit) for punch without constant low end. — [T](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tUQzWaODcek) **[55k, single channel. The genre recipe is plausible but not verified. The "uncompressed overhead / saturation over compression" idea is treated as a stylistic reference only.]**

**Overheads (often off for this band)**
- OH HPF high: about 200 Hz per [D](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SY-BnLQNwOc) and 160 Hz per [E](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FIlwKWlfXc0). **[VERIFIED: 2 channels; 222k+91k]**. OH compression 4:1, attack about 15 ms, hold 0 — [B](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WxOnpsuAsqw) **[215k]**. A fast attack and release on the OH takes the snare transient out of the OH so the OH can be raised for cymbals — [C](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CshdvNnDKr4) **[145k]**.

### Inferences
- For Sunrise Jam's single kick mic, a chain that fits the verified numbers is:
  - HPF about 30–38 Hz.
  - Gate with range about 12–20 dB, attack about 0.5 ms, hold about 40–60 ms, release about 10–100 ms. Use expander mode, or a longer release, for a less clipped funk kick.
  - EQ: cut about 118–285 Hz, boost 2–4 kHz or high-band click.
  - Compressor: 4–5:1, attack 25–30 ms, release 70–110 ms, 2–4 dB GR.
- Snare: no gate; HPF about 100 Hz; −2 to −4 dB at about 420 Hz; +2.5 kHz and +8 kHz boosts; compressor 4:1, attack about 10–15 ms, release about 50–80 ms, 3–6 dB GR to lift ghost notes. The broadcast has no feedback loop, so ghost-note lift via compression and an upward expander is low-risk.

### Gaps
- No high-view video demonstrates a funk/neo-soul-specific live snare chain with numbers. Ghost-note handling is inferred from general live tutorials (C, E, A).
- No verified numbers were found for an expander ratio, for example 1:2–1:3. Sources say "expander" without settings.

## 2. Bass DI and synth bass: compression stages, EQ for phone/laptop translation, saturation, avoiding mud with the kick, making two bass sources coexist

### Takeaway
The bass DI consensus is: HPF about 40 Hz (not higher), optional LPF or shelf about 5–6 kHz for string noise, and compression 4:1 with attack 10–20 ms, release 100–250 ms, roughly 6–10 dB GR. Sources say compress bass hard. Definition and small-speaker translation come from midrange: about 200 Hz psychoacoustic fullness, 400–800 Hz notes and definition, 2–4 kHz attack. Saturation adds harmonics. Mud is cut at about 240–550 Hz. To coexist, give each source a job and a frequency slot. For a synth bass alongside a real bass, cut the synth's low-mids and focus its push at about 50–60 Hz while shelving below (Maserati).

### Cited Findings
**HPF and LPF**
- HPF about 40 Hz on bass:
  - "I'm going to go with 40 Hz… lowest note on a four-string is about 40 Hz" — [H](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FNa7avcUUSM)
  - "high-pass filter at about 40 Hz helps the subharmonics not make it flabby… 41 Hz is the lowest note" — [G](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ctBDFPnhrOs)
  - "put it really low like at 40 Hz" — [I](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DDhDnuJXHHc)
  - Andy Wallace chain filtered at about 40 Hz — [J](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zrWA9rglG24)
  - **[VERIFIED: 4 channels; 415k+198k+160k+113k]**
- Low-pass or high shelf to remove string and fret noise: LPF about 6 kHz ("not much usable energy above 6k") per [H](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FNa7avcUUSM); LPF for "clang" per [G](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ctBDFPnhrOs); high shelf down for string noise per [I](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DDhDnuJXHHc). **[VERIFIED: 3 channels]**

**Compression**
- 3–4:1, soft knee, **attack about 20 ms** ("too fast thins out the bass"), release 200–500 ms (250 ms chosen for fast notes), "aim for about 5 to 10 dB of compression". Pro tip: **HPF on the compressor sidechain** so low notes are not over-squashed. — [G](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ctBDFPnhrOs) **[VERIFIED: 198k]**
- 4:1, attack 10 ms, hold 0, release 100 ms, makeup +6 dB, settled at about 9 dB GR (12 dB was too much). — [I](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DDhDnuJXHHc) **[VERIFIED: 113k]**
- Ratio 4:1 up to 10:1, slower attack to keep the pluck, quicker release, short hold. **The XR18 "Bass" compressor preset has 6 dB makeup built in, so pull the threshold down until about 6 dB of average GR.** — [H](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FNa7avcUUSM) **[VERIFIED: 160k; directly relevant to MR18/XR18]**
- Wallace chain: 4:1 SSL channel compressor — [J](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zrWA9rglG24). "Don't be afraid to compress a bass guitar hard" — [U3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N78qMdD0sBo) and [G](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ctBDFPnhrOs). **[ratio 4:1 consensus: 4 channels]**
- Barresi: "nine out of ten times" an 1176 (black/rev-E style, less bright than the blue) on bass DI for control, or LA-2A on ballads. The DI is "75% of the bass sound". — [Joe Barresi, Waves Audio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Cmhd8fZ28fk) **[327k]**

**EQ for definition and translation**
- Midrange for audibility and definition, not lows:
  - "Resist the urge to boost lows… boost around 800 Hz a few dB"; string attack at 2–4 kHz; cut mud around 250 Hz (sweep 200–500) — [H](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FNa7avcUUSM)
  - Boost about 200 Hz "tricks your ear" via the missing fundamental, helping sub-challenged systems; 400–500 Hz for note definition and growl — [G](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ctBDFPnhrOs)
  - Scheps finds a "honky", wah-like midrange where "you really hear the notes", plus a broad about 100 Hz lift (Helios-style) — [K](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BMW4K7FzQp8)
  - **[VERIFIED: 3 channels; 219k+198k+160k]**
- Low-mid mud cut about 240–550 Hz: Wallace −1.6 dB at 240 Hz per [J](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zrWA9rglG24); 250 Hz (200–500 sweep) per [H](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FNa7avcUUSM); about 550 Hz on X32 per [U3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N78qMdD0sBo). **[VERIFIED: 3 channels]**
- Wallace "big but controlled" trick: a cranked 1073 low shelf at 35 Hz (closest to the 40 Hz fundamental), then HPF about 40 Hz plus a 240 Hz cut plus 4:1 compression. — [J](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zrWA9rglG24) **[415k; single source for the boost-then-filter detail]**

**Saturation**
- Saturation adds harmonics (about 2×, 4×, 8× the fundamental) that phones can reproduce, and the ear infers the missing fundamental. Run it at about 25% wet/dry, or on a duplicated parallel "distorted bass" fader that can itself be EQ'd and compressed. Check on a full-range system so it does not overload. — [Q](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7q1PeGsmhn8) (41k)
- Saturators and exciters on kick and bass generate higher harmonics that separate the two instruments with less low-frequency phase interference. A mild transient expander emphasizes the first about 50 ms where their highs live. — [L](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=27ewHn5SAiI) (243k)
- Maserati adds distortion and tape saturation to a bass for "energy". — [M](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x8uzol0NixE) (144k)
- Overall: **[VERIFIED: 3 channels; 243k+144k+41k]**

**Synth bass alongside real bass**
- Maserati (synth bass in a track with a real bass): EQ **before** the compressor. Reduce the low-mids and add some high-mid to the synth so "it's not stepping on the real bass". Focus the low push "around that 50 to 60 Hz area and get rid of things below it", with the EQ'd low end driving the compressor so it pumps with the beat. Bypassing the EQ makes it "fill up more space… getting in the way". — [M](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x8uzol0NixE) **[144k]**
- Role definition: "the kick needs to be a very consistent bottom… the bass will be more of a melodic low end" — [K](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BMW4K7FzQp8). "Kick drum provides the punch and the bass guitar provides the sustain" — [G](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ctBDFPnhrOs). **[VERIFIED: 2 channels; 219k+198k]**
- Scheps puts low-end glue on the **mix bus** (Pultec boosting at 100 Hz, about "3") rather than on each instrument. — [K](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BMW4K7FzQp8) (219k)

### Inferences
- MR18 bass DI chain consistent with the verified findings:
  - HPF 35–40 Hz.
  - EQ: −2 dB at about 250 Hz (Q about 1.5); +2 dB at about 700–800 Hz; +2 dB at about 2.5 kHz if more pluck is needed; high shelf −3 dB at 5–6 kHz.
  - Compressor: 4:1, attack 10–20 ms, release 100–250 ms, 6–9 dB GR.
  - Light parallel saturation, about 25% wet, for phones.
- Synth bass DI (sub plus bass layer):
  - HPF about 25–30 Hz.
  - Let it own 40–60 Hz when it is the sub role, and cut it at about 200–500 Hz where the electric bass carries the notes.
  - Its definition comes from saturation harmonics, not low-mid boosts.
  - When both play together, the bass guitar carries 100 Hz to 1 kHz and the synth carries the sub.
- This split is extrapolated from Maserati [M] and Scheps [K]. No source gave exact numbers for a sub-bass vs bass-guitar crossover.

### Gaps
- No YouTube source found with a specific "synth-bass sub + bass guitar" live split (for example, synth LPF at 120 Hz and bass HPF at 60 Hz). This is inference only.
- Saturation drive numbers specific to MR18/X32 FX (for example, a tape or "Enhancer" FX setting) were not found in a verified source.

## 3. Kick vs bass vs synth-bass carving: sidechain / ducking / dynamic EQ

### Takeaway
On X32/M32/XR18-family consoles, the documented live technique is a **gate in DUCK mode on the bass**, keyed from the kick channel with a key filter near the kick fundamental. Shallow ducking of about 4 dB is enough. Static carving (kick cut about 118 Hz to make room for the bass) and harmonic separation via saturation are the high-view complements.

### Cited Findings
- X32 ducker recipe on the bass channel:
  - Gate mode = DUCK, key source = kick channel, key filter about **58 Hz** (kick fundamental 55–60 Hz).
  - **Range about 4 dB**, attack about **10 ms** (faster gives more kick impact but an "abrupt" bass), hold about **50 ms**, release about **132 ms** ("about the length of our kick drum").
  - At 20 dB of range "this is obviously too much".
  - Source: [N](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Csmtq0WGPcI) (74k, single channel; below the 100k bar. The *principle* of carving the kick against the bass is corroborated by K, G, L. The exact numbers are listed as single-source.)
- Static carving: Scheps cuts the kick at about 118 Hz "leaving more room for the bass". — [K](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BMW4K7FzQp8) **[219k]**
- Low-end interference physics: notes within about 6% of each other at E1 (41.2 Hz) are only about 2.4 Hz apart, causing beating and cancellation. Suggestions: attenuate kick overtones that collide with the bass fundamental; use saturation, exciters or transient expansion to separate in the upper range. — [L](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=27ewHn5SAiI) **[243k]**
- Voodoo kick multiband expander (50–100 Hz and 100–175 Hz bands that open only on hits) acts as a frequency-specific dynamic carve. — [T](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tUQzWaODcek) (55k)

### Inferences
- The broadcast server is not limited to MR18 tools. A reasonable server-side version of N is a sidechain dynamic EQ band on the bass DI (and on the synth bass), keyed from the kick: about 55–80 Hz, −3 to −4 dB, attack about 10 ms, release about 130 ms. This keeps the kick and bass lows unfiltered, per the band rule. It is the dynamic equivalent of N's ducker with only the low band affected (inference).

### Gaps
- No ≥100k YouTube video was found with numeric dynamic-EQ sidechain settings for kick vs bass in a live context. MusicRadar's "Blending kicks and bass with sidechained dynamic EQ" (rUxWvtBfCSo) was found but not transcribed.

## 4. Congas (two mics): HPF, EQ, compression, panning

### Takeaway
Two channels agree that conga attack and slap live about 3.5–5 kHz. Fundamentals are about 200 Hz (low drum) and about 270 Hz (higher drum). There is a mid "body" or tone around 900 Hz–1 kHz, and a hand "snap" around 1.5–2 kHz. Pan to make a "conversation": the main drum near center, slightly off; the low drum about 9 o'clock; the other opposite. No compression numbers were found.

### Cited Findings
- JC Losada: the quinto (main conga) is the "snare and kick" of the percussion set. Emphasize the low and low-mid palm hit (beats 1 and 3) and the **slap at 3.5–4.5 kHz**. Tumba (low): careful with low-mids ("gets muddy"), emphasize **4–5 kHz** so the head hit reads. Mid conga: emphasize **about 900 Hz–1 kHz** plus top end. Panning: main conga near center, slightly left; tumba about 9 o'clock; the other conga on the opposite side. — [R](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oLDVLFYGjQ8) (44k)
- Marco Galvan: fundamentals at **about 200 Hz** (tumba) and **about 270 Hz** (conga), found as two analyzer peaks and boosted or cut to taste. Hand **snap about 1.5–2 kHz**; **brightness about 5 kHz**. — [S](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ASFeqBOGk2Q) (16k)
- Agreement on the attack/brightness region of about 4–5 kHz: **[VERIFIED: 2 channels; 44k+16k]**. The panning and 900 Hz–1 kHz details are single-source (R).

### Inferences
- For two conga mics on a broadcast:
  - HPF about 80–100 Hz. This sits below the about 200 Hz fundamental, following the "HPF just under the lowest fundamental" rule from [D](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SY-BnLQNwOc).
  - Partial pans of about L30/R30 (or as above).
  - +2–3 dB at about 4–5 kHz.
  - Control mud at 200–400 Hz.
  - Light compression borrowed from the snare numbers (4:1, about 15 ms attack, 50–100 ms release, 2–4 dB GR) to bring out ghost strokes, which R says must be audible.
- None of these compression numbers come from a conga-specific source.

### Gaps
- No conga compression numbers found. Losada's full masterclass (compression section) is behind waves.com, and ES Mixing's conga EQ video (0M5zBV9nQlA, 11.5k) had no transcript available.
- No live-sound (X32/MR18) conga tutorial with numbers was found.

## 5. Drum bus / parallel compression numbers

### Takeaway
Parallel drum compression is verified by two channels, including CLA at 153k. On X32-family consoles, Drew Brashler sends **shells only (no cymbals)** to a second stereo subgroup. Settings: 10:1, attack 10 ms, release 70 ms, about 12 dB GR, EQ after the compressor (low shelf boost about 150 Hz, +4 dB high shelf at 10 kHz, cut about 400 Hz), blended under the main drum bus. CLA uses a gentle 2:1 with 100 ms release, adds +3 dB at 60 Hz and +3 dB at 8 kHz on the return, and does not stereo-link.

### Cited Findings
- X32 recipe:
  - Buses 7–8 = drum subgroup; buses 9–10 = parallel subgroup fed only by kick, snare and toms; channels unassigned from LR.
  - Dynamics placed **before** EQ on the parallel bus.
  - Compressor: **10:1, attack 10 ms, hold 0, release about 70 ms, about 12 dB GR, makeup about 5 dB**.
  - EQ: low shelf boost "about 150 and below", **high shelf at 10 kHz +4 dB**, cut about **400 Hz**.
  - Caveat: inserting an FX-rack compressor (Ultimo) adds latency only on that bus, so the same FX must also be inserted on the dry bus to stay time-aligned.
  - Source: [O](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C3K60IS8Pj4) (52k)
- CLA: Neve 33609 style, threshold 0, **recovery 100 ms, ratio 2:1**, **not stereo-linked** ("if you link it it makes it sound more mono"). Return EQ **+3 dB at 8 kHz, +3 dB at 60 Hz**. — [P](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z0eqw_3cY2A) (153k)
- Agreement on a parallel compressed drum return EQ'd with low plus high boosts: **[VERIFIED: 2 channels; 153k+52k]**. The ratio and GR differ widely (10:1 at about 12 dB vs 2:1). Treat the ratio as a style choice: "smash" vs "glue".

### Inferences
- For the cloud mix, where latency is not an issue if the plugin host compensates:
  - Shells-only parallel bus: about 8–10:1, attack about 10 ms, release about 70 ms, 10–12 dB GR.
  - Post-comp EQ +3 dB at 60 Hz, −2 dB at 400 Hz, +3–4 dB at 8–10 kHz.
  - Blend about 6–10 dB under the dry drum bus.
- For a dry Voodoo/Vulfpeck aesthetic, keep the blend lower and the overhead uncompressed, per [T](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tUQzWaODcek).

### Gaps
- No verified video gave drum *bus* (glue) compression numbers for a dry funk kit, as distinct from parallel. "Parallel Compression | FOH Masterclass ft Robert Scovill" (SzS0RkRLUfY, 21k) and Ascension Worship (dAoiGeSdH2s, 27k) were found but not transcribed.
