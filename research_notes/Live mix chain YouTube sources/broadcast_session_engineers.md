## Q1. How is a broadcast mix built differently from FOH? Audience and ambience mics: placement, level, compression, riding between songs

### Takeaway
Feed the stream from a separate stereo send that is not touched by the PA's room EQ. Add audience mics to that send only, filter them hard (high-pass about 200–300 Hz, low-pass about 10 kHz), keep them hard-panned in stereo pairs, keep them low under the music ("sprinkles, not the cake"), and push them up between songs and for applause. TV A1s (the lead broadcast sound mixer) ride them all the time. On an unmanned rig, a sidechain duck keyed from the drums does the same job. If you raise them much, time-align them to the board feed so they don't flam.

### Cited Findings
**Verified**
- **Feed the stream a "clean" L/R, separate from the PA processing.** Vulfpeck's touring FOH engineer does his PA EQ on a matrix after L/R, "so I can adjust the EQ of the PA… without affecting the stream mix. So the stream mix is getting their own clean version of my left right mix." — [He mixes Vulfpeck live — Dallas Taylor — 305k views](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g-HC64mO9-Q). Church broadcast setups do the same thing with a post-fader stereo stream bus that has its own sends-on-fader levels, e.g. pastor/host mics "bumped… like 6 dB" higher in the stream than in the house — [Ryland Russell, 36k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YM3FiDcTJRU); post-fade broadcast bus 13/14 set at exactly 0.0 dB into USB/OBS — [Ascension Worship, 45k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XpkYd09WFXc). [3 channels; 305k+36k+45k]
- **Why audience/room mics are needed at all:** close-miked/IEM stages sound "like singing in a tin can" on stream. Room mics bring back reflections and the crowd — [Attaway Audio, 56k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SFEVZkiS0J4); [Collaborate Worship, 78k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2NyFLUsz0MA); [Ballast Media, 90k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rOSAymbwsCY) ("helps with that direct harshness… from your front-of-house mixer"). [3 channels; 56k+78k+90k]
- **Placement options**, with the same three positions named on several channels: (a) on stage facing the audience. Shotguns are best hung high near the stage front and angled down about one-third of the way into the crowd, so the back lobe rejects drums and wedges. (b) Hung over the audience, e.g. choir mics or truss. (c) At FOH/the back of the room, e.g. omnis, or a pair pointed at the PA for "more energy". Use several spread-out pairs that hear different groups of people rather than one pair turned up loud, because one pair turned up "you're gonna hear a bunch of your PA instead" — [Attaway Audio, 56k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SFEVZkiS0J4); [Ballast Media, 90k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rOSAymbwsCY); [Collaborate Worship/BridgeAVL, 24k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EbJcAQSXKf8). [3 channels]
- **Audience-mic filtering:** "low cut filter at about 300 hertz" — [Collaborate Worship, 78k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2NyFLUsz0MA); "extreme high-pass filter set between 200 and 300 Hertz… go crazy with your high-pass", plus small notches at 250 Hz and 500 Hz for wash and boxiness — [Ballast Media, 90k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rOSAymbwsCY); "roll up the high pass" and "roll off some of the top end" on FOH ambience mics — [Attaway Audio, 56k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SFEVZkiS0J4); "high cut… about 10K and above and the low cut is about 300 and below" to remove PA sibilance — [Collaborate Worship/BridgeAVL, 24k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EbJcAQSXKf8); Warren Huart: "a lot of low mids that I pulled out" of the room mics — [Produce Like A Pro, 66k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y1q5e3jYfZc). [4 channels agree on HPF around 200–300 Hz]
- **Stereo: link or pan the pairs hard L/R** so they stay out of the busy center and add width — [Ballast Media, 90k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rOSAymbwsCY) ("each pair is panned hard left and right"); [Collaborate Worship, 78k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2NyFLUsz0MA); [Attaway, 56k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SFEVZkiS0J4) ("mono is fine… but stereo and spread out is way better"). [3 channels]
- **Keep them low or sparing, or you get echo and flams:** "they're the sprinkles not the cake". Timing differences cause "two different hits on the snare drum". You can delay the direct signal to match — [Attaway, 56k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SFEVZkiS0J4). With heavy kicks, room mics produce "this terrible flam between the live kick… and the front of house live kick", so be surgical and ride them — [Produce Like A Pro, 66k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y1q5e3jYfZc). Turned up too much they sound "echoey" — [Collaborate Worship, 78k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2NyFLUsz0MA). [3 channels]
- **Time alignment (delay the board mix, not the mics):** treat the furthest audience pair as "point zero that everything gets delayed to". Record a balloon pop through the stage mic and the audience mics, then delay the board/stream feed until the transients line up. In their room that was 27.9 ft, confirmed with a laser measure. After alignment, "you can turn these audience mics up and you're going to hear better clapping" — [Collaborate Worship/BridgeAVL, 24k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EbJcAQSXKf8). Delaying the direct signal is also named as an option by [Attaway, 56k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SFEVZkiS0J4). [2 channels]
- **Ride the audience mics between songs and for applause.** SNL's A1 Bob Paladino uses 25–30 audience mics (Shures and Sennheiser mini shotguns) on a fader "set at zero" and rides them with a foot "gas pedal", because "you have to ride applause. You can't just leave it one place" — [SNL, Dallas Taylor, 112k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tlmK-QCLthI). In a live-recording mix: "Don't be afraid to pull them up and down where needed… I'm pulling them up there because [of the cheers]" — [Produce Like A Pro, 66k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y1q5e3jYfZc). On an unmanned stream: crowd mics are compressed by a sidechain keyed from the drum bus, ducking about 15 dB, "so the crowd mics are not in whenever the drums are in". Use "really long" hold and release so the crowd swells up smoothly when the drums stop — [Ryland Russell, 36k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YM3FiDcTJRU). [3 channels agree on riding up between songs; the drum-keyed duck itself comes from 1 channel]
- **Light compression on audience mics:** SNL uses "very light compression on the individual mics and then… a little bit on the main bus… it barely moves" — [SNL, 112k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tlmK-QCLthI). Ballast puts no processing on the individual channels and does it all on one audience bus (EQ → Waves L2 to raise the level and catch peaks → a light widener), because it "helps all the mics sound like one crowd" — [Ballast Media, 90k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rOSAymbwsCY). [2 channels]
- **Tiny Desk uses an elevated ambience/audience head:** Josh Rogosin hung a Neumann KU 100 binaural head "about 9 ft in the air" above the desk for "audience action and applause". He rolled off low end on it for air-conditioning noise — [Produce Like A Pro, 28k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lvgipZcIbAg). The Tiny Desk itself started as one stereo (M/S shotgun) mic, augmented by spot mics and DIs — [NPR Music, 796k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e07bI5rz6FY). [Core M/S-plus-spots approach verified by 796k. The KU 100 detail comes from 1 channel at 28k]
- **Don't send audience mics to the PA** (feedback). They can double as IEM ambience — [Collaborate Worship, 78k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2NyFLUsz0MA); [Ryland Russell, 36k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YM3FiDcTJRU) (stream bus and IEMs only); the Vulfpeck monitor engineer also adds ambient mics to IEMs so they "sound natural" — [Dallas Taylor, 305k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g-HC64mO9-Q). [3 channels]

**Unverified (single channel, <100k views)**
- Starting level for ambient mics in the stream mix: "a good starting point is negative 10 dB", then adjust by ear — [Collaborate Worship, 78k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2NyFLUsz0MA).
- Small-diaphragm cardioid condensers a few feet behind the mains on each side of the stage, at the height of the performers — [Collaborate Worship, 78k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2NyFLUsz0MA).
- Watch for HVAC noise from mics hung in the ceiling, and for open FOH mics catching private conversation — [Attaway, 56k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SFEVZkiS0J4).
- Sidechain-gate automation on crowd mics: same idea as Ryland Russell's, found only as a video title, with no transcript reviewed — [AllamHouse, 8k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5EecCtcsM_A).

### Inferences
- For Sunrise Jam's cloud chain, which mixes from the raw multitrack: the "clean L/R separate from PA EQ" principle is automatic. The MR18 room or ambient channels can be high-passed around 250–300 Hz, low-passed around 10 kHz, hard-panned, and ducked from a drum-bus sidechain with long release so they swell between songs. Because the server receives the multitrack, the alignment delay can be applied to the band bus (a few ms per metre of mic-to-source distance) rather than with balloon pops on the console.
- If there are no real audience mics, the sources point to a reverb send a little hotter than FOH (see Q2) as the stand-in.

### Gaps
- No KEXP, Audiotree, BBC/Jools Holland, Glastonbury or Coachella A1 video gave numeric audience-mic levels or compressor settings. Searches surfaced only performance uploads, no engineer interviews with transcripts.
- No source gave dB numbers for how far to push audience mics between songs relative to during songs, apart from the roughly 15 dB sidechain duck (1 channel).

## Q2. Overall balance philosophy (vocal on top, bass/kick, keys in the low mids), bus/glue compression, reverb, stereo image

### Takeaway
The vocal is ridden and kept intelligible by riding and leveling (multiband compression and de-essing), not by gating out bleed. Featured solos and fills get pushed up as they happen. Bus compression is light (about 2:1, a couple of dB, "barely moves"). Live mixes take somewhat more reverb and delay than studio mixes, and the effects are ridden musically. For width, keys and synths go L/R so the bass and vocal own the center.

### Cited Findings
**Verified**
- **Intelligibility of every lead vocal is the goal. Ride vocals and solos all show long.** "My main job during the show is to ride the solo instruments and then the vocals." Chain: HPF/LPF → gate → EQ → compressor → multiband compressor ("when they sing really low, it will compress just the low end… This will help with S's") — [He mixes Vulfpeck live, 305k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g-HC64mO9-Q). Audiotree uses a multiband with the low band catching pops and the top band de-essing, then a "leveling compressor" so faders don't have to move as far — [Audiotree, 14k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Aj_YsEFMto). Warren Huart puts a vocal rider plus gentle de-essing, EQ/compress/EQ/compress in "small amounts" on a live-recording vocal — [Produce Like A Pro, 66k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y1q5e3jYfZc). Ryland Russell uses an extra bus comp "just knocking off a couple of dB… so you're not having to worry about riding faders as much" — [Ryland Russell, 36k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YM3FiDcTJRU). [4 channels; 305k+]
- **Push featured moments.** Rogosin: "if there's a little bass lick even if it's in the middle of a verse I'll… force it to pop out of the mix" — [Produce Like A Pro, 28k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lvgipZcIbAg). He takes a bass-amp direct out "so if your bass player has a little solo you'll have… more control to ride that level" — [NPR Music, 796k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e07bI5rz6FY). Nic Hard automates extra EQ and compression on for solos (bass solo, Cory Henry synth solo, trumpet solo brightened) — [SonicScoop, 66k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bNQkbY7A9XY). [3 channels; 796k]
- **Embrace bleed; don't gate drums or chase cymbal bleed out of the vocal.** Audiotree: "we don't get rid of it, we actually embrace bleed… room bleed makes depth" — [Audiotree, 14k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Aj_YsEFMto). Rick Fritz on gating drums: "I am NOT gating… it just fails miserably" — [Audiotree Q&A](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yysOPSPNL9U). Warren Huart: over-processing bleed in the vocal makes "the cymbals… really loud then are out", so "control the volume of the vocal more evenly" — [Produce Like A Pro, 66k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y1q5e3jYfZc). Nic Hard prefers editing or transient designers to gates because "gates don't always open in time" — [SonicScoop, 30k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0bR1n88OIDg). [3 channels]
- **Bus/glue compression is light.** SNL: "a little bit on the main bus… it barely moves… we keep the show pretty open… dynamic" — [SNL, 112k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tlmK-QCLthI). Nic Hard (Snarky Puppy mix bus): "SSL bus compressor… super light compression". Drum bus: Townhouse comp "attack of 10, super fast release… 2:1 ratio", a 63 Hz bump from an SSL drum strip, an "API 550A… knocking out 2 dB at 400 Hz", tape emulation, and a cut above 18 kHz — [SonicScoop, 66k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bNQkbY7A9XY). Audiotree masters live with Ozone "to get it up to broadcast level while maintaining… as much of the dynamics as we can" — [Audiotree, 32k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r4tzdMyJyck). [3 channels agree on "light/open"; the specific numbers come from Nic Hard only]
- **A live mix takes a little more reverb and delay, ridden musically.** Warren Huart: "I'm using louder reverbs and delays… than I normally would because it's live… Don't be afraid" — [Produce Like A Pro, 66k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y1q5e3jYfZc). Ryland Russell sets the stream mix's reverb sends "a little bit hotter" than the house: "a good blend of crowd mic and reverb makes for a more live feel" — [Ryland Russell, 36k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YM3FiDcTJRU). Vulfpeck FOH: vocal delay "I'm riding that musically… If the song doesn't call for it, I'm not going to use it" — [Dallas Taylor, 305k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g-HC64mO9-Q). AudioHaze on Vulf videos: reverb and slapback automated phrase to phrase ("completely dry… then super verbed out") — [AudioHaze, 77k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ia49mOJCCzQ). [4 channels; 305k]
- **Reverb type:** Ryland Russell prefers a "vintage room" over a plate on vocals, with a separate set-and-forget drum reverb — [36k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YM3FiDcTJRU). Nic Hard uses AltiVerb (plates/default patches) and puts reverb on horns "to set them back… so they're not… dry and impersonal" — [SonicScoop, 66k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bNQkbY7A9XY). Rogosin often adds no reverb and lets the real room carry it ("I really didn't add any Reverb… the room was stunning") — [Produce Like A Pro, 28k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lvgipZcIbAg). [3 channels, but they disagree on type. The consensus is only that a room-like ambience beats an obvious effect]
- **Tiny Desk balance philosophy:** balance the band acoustically "as if you're all sitting around a campfire" — [NPR Music, 97k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EFiJHEYomqo). Then apply "pretty heavy amounts of EQ that really make everything pop": kick thump, tom "boom", cymbal sizzle — [NPR Music, 346k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G7L5cLqdRwE). DI blended with mics on acoustic instruments for articulation — [NPR Music, 796k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e07bI5rz6FY). [NPR, 796k/346k]
- **Stereo image: hard-panned, wide room/ambience pairs and a clear center** — see Q1 (3 channels). Ryland Russell also uses a stereo enhancer on the stream bus, "spread things a little bit but not too much" — [36k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YM3FiDcTJRU). Ballast puts a widener on the audience bus — [90k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rOSAymbwsCY). [2 channels for light widening on the stream or audience bus]

**Unverified (single channel, <100k views)**
- Keys/synth "faux stereo": a stereo delay of **0 ms on one side and 20 ms on the other** on mono clav and bass synth, plus a Dimension-D-style widener on other synths, "so that there's space for the bass guitar in the middle" — [Nic Hard, SonicScoop, 66k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bNQkbY7A9XY).
- Keys in the low mids: Nic Hard high-passes the Rhodes "up to the lowest frequency that you hear in the Rhodes" and cuts some top, so it "exists in the mid-range", the space carved out of the drums with the −2 dB at 400 Hz. B3 low-cut to its lowest played note, with a Distressor taking "a couple of dB" off the loud parts. LA-2A on Rhodes and bass bus; DI plus amp-sim blend on bass — [SonicScoop, 66k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bNQkbY7A9XY).
- Kick/bass split: "Kick sits about… 40 to 60, bass about 80 to 100", plus bass high-mid boosts for definition (around 800 Hz) and parallel compression (MV2) — [Warren Huart, Produce Like A Pro, 66k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y1q5e3jYfZc).
- Audiotree vocal mics HPF "around 250 to 300 Hz" in a loud, bleed-heavy room — [Audiotree, 14k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Aj_YsEFMto).
- Least favorite frequency on guitar amps: about 2.5 kHz — [Rick Fritz, Audiotree Q&A](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yysOPSPNL9U).
- Pastor/host (MC) mics +6 dB in the stream mix compared with the house — [Ryland Russell, 36k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YM3FiDcTJRU). This is the only MC-level number found. No video covered MC ducking of the music.

### Inferences
- No source gave a numeric "vocal X dB above the band" rule. Every engineer instead describes riding and leveling the vocal (and the solo of the moment) so it is always intelligible. The earlier report's "3–7 dB on top" figure remains unsourced by YouTube.
- For Sunrise Jam: bass in the center, Rhodes and keys high-passed to their lowest note and panned or Haas-widened off center, a light 2:1 drum-bus comp with a small low-mid dip around 400 Hz, and a barely-moving mix-bus comp. All of these match the Nic Hard and SNL practice above.

### Gaps
- Nothing found from KEXP (Kevin Suggs), Hiatus Kaiyote engineers, BBC/Jools Holland, or festival A1s on balance numbers.
- No source gave a numeric vocal-over-band offset or a mix-bus ratio/threshold besides Nic Hard's drum bus.

## Q3. Numeric settings for stream output loudness and limiting (LUFS, ceiling, attack/release)

### Takeaway
Put a brick-wall limiter last on the stream bus. Ceiling −1 dBFS (verified). Raise the input gain until gain reduction reaches about 3–4 dB on the loudest parts, with fast attack and roughly 100–150 ms release. The only explicit loudness target heard was −16 LUFS (109k views). Pushing harder causes pumping and exposes snare brightness and low-end distortion.

### Cited Findings
**Verified**
- **Output ceiling −1 dB:** "set output gain to negative 1 dB" — [Collaborate Worship, 100k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5jA7-GLzt0M); output "−1… so even if an inter-sample peak squeezes by" — [Ascension Worship, 45k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XpkYd09WFXc); "output gain at negative one… brick wall" — [Ryland Russell, 36k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YM3FiDcTJRU). Drew Brashler uses −0.5 in the demo but recommends −5 dBFS for safety — [109k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2jhggSKR2AI). [3 channels at −1; 1 differs]
- **Gain reduction about 3–4 dB max:** "gain reduction meter… no more than three or four dB… higher than that… sucking in" — [Collaborate Worship, 100k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5jA7-GLzt0M); "about 3 dB of… limiting" — [Ascension, 45k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XpkYd09WFXc); "knock about another 3 dB" — [Ryland Russell, 36k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YM3FiDcTJRU). [3 channels]
- **Limiter input gain starting around +12 dB** (X32 Precision Limiter, then trim): [Collaborate Worship, 100k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5jA7-GLzt0M); [Drew Brashler, 109k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2jhggSKR2AI). [2 channels]
- **Attack and release:** attack 0.5 ms, release 150 ms, knee 3, squeeze 20% — [Collaborate Worship, 100k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5jA7-GLzt0M). Attack 0.1 ms, release 96 ms ("100 ms or under"), knee 0, squeeze 60% — [Drew Brashler, 109k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2jhggSKR2AI). Default 662 ms release is "pretty smooth"; shorter is louder and more aggressive — [Ascension, 45k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XpkYd09WFXc). [2 high-view channels agree on fast attack ≤0.5 ms and release of about 100–150 ms]
- **Loudness target:** "for most of our streaming platforms, we should be targeting −16 LUFS" — [Drew Brashler, 109k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2jhggSKR2AI). ## Q4. Neo-soul/funk tone references from these engineers

### Takeaway
The Vulf/Tiny Desk funk sound uses few mics and lots of DI. Dynamic mics are recorded hot and compressed hard, the top end is often rolled off, guitar and bass stay dry and immediate, the drum information is minimal, and vocal slap and reverb are automated by phrase. Snarky Puppy (Nic Hard) keeps the keyboards in the mids and on the sides so the bass owns the center.

### Cited Findings
**Verified**
- **DI-heavy instruments blended with or replacing mics:** Rogosin takes a DI or direct out on bass amps and acoustic instruments to blend for articulation — [NPR Music, 796k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e07bI5rz6FY). Vulf sessions: "all bass, guitar, and keys… DI"; guitar and bass "pretty dry direct sounds… as much immediacy as possible" — [AudioHaze, 77k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ia49mOJCCzQ). Nic Hard uses DI plus an amp sim for bass and a DI-only clav — [SonicScoop, 66k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bNQkbY7A9XY). [3 channels; 796k]
- **Groove with minimal drum information:** Jack Stratton: "my latest MO is… trying to groove with as little drum information as possible" — [Vulf, Don't Steal This Groove](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VzGGTAGT0RQ). AudioHaze counts 2–3 mics on Vulf drums (an off-axis SM57 for the kick, plus snare) recorded hot and compressed hard to bring out the room — [AudioHaze, 77k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ia49mOJCCzQ). Tiny Desk uses a Glyn Johns-style pair 30–40 in from the snare center, plus a kick mic — [NPR Music, 346k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G7L5cLqdRwE). [3 channels on the "few drum mics" approach]
- **Mixing philosophy:** Jack Stratton: mixing is "the most important thing… and the least important thing", meaning great performance first, and drums drive mixing breakthroughs — [Vulf, 193k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WZviVJfepDM). The Vulfpeck FOH engineer: the band "mix themselves almost" — [Dallas Taylor, 305k](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g-HC64mO9-Q). [2 channels, both >100k]

**Unverified**
- Stratton's funk-guitar chain: an EMT-style delay at low wet, into an LA-2A on the way in. Doubled guitars panned L/R and sent to an aux bus with Goodhertz Tone Control, the "Vulf Compressor" on the "punch and crunch" preset, and a focusing EQ with some high end removed. Rhythm-machine snare pitched down, "Prince"-style — [Vulf, views not captured](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VzGGTAGT0RQ).

### Inferences
- For a neo-soul/funk livestream, the agreed-upon moves are: keep bass/guitar/keys DI-forward and dry, compress drums and room characterfully rather than gating, automate vocal slap and reverb to the phrasing, and push solos as they happen.
